### EQ-FILT-LORENZ-001 — Lorenz-63 system

`dx/dt = sigma (y - x)`, `dy/dt = x (rho - z) - y`, `dz/dt = x y - beta z`;
classical parameters `sigma = 10`, `rho = 28`, `beta = 8/3`. Fixed points
`(0, 0, 0)` and, for `rho > 1`,
`(+-sqrt(beta (rho - 1)), +-sqrt(beta (rho - 1)), rho - 1)`.

- Domain: real `sigma`, `rho`, `beta`; the state-space transition advances
  the system by `substeps` classical Runge-Kutta steps of size `step`
  ([EQ-NUM-ODE-003]); the default step `0.01` is the step of Lorenz (1963),
  who integrated with a second-order scheme.
- Meaning: a truncated convection model whose classical parameters give a
  chaotic attractor; the standard three-variable test of nonlinear filters.
- Code: `filtering.lorenz63_rhs`, `filtering.lorenz63`.
- Source: [REF-LORENZ-1963, Equations 25-27 and Section 7].

## `probstat.filtering.kalman`

### EQ-FILT-BAYES-001 — Sequential Bayesian filtering recursion

Prediction (Chapman-Kolmogorov):
`p(x_k|y_1:k-1) = integral p(x_k|x_(k-1)) p(x_(k-1)|y_1:k-1) dx_(k-1)`;
update (Bayes): `p(x_k|y_1:k) ∝ p(y_k|x_k) p(x_k|y_1:k-1)`.

- Domain: Markov states and observations conditionally independent given the
  current state.
- Meaning: every filter in the library is an exact or approximate
  implementation of this two-step recursion; smoothing additionally conditions
  on future data.
- Code: `filtering.kalman_filter`, `filtering.bootstrap_particle_filter`,
  `data_assimilation.ensemble_kalman_filter`.
- Source: [REF-SARKKA-2013, Chapter 4].
  - [REF-SARKKA-2013](../references.md#ref-sarkka-2013): Simo Särkkä. *Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing*. Institute of Mathematical Statistics Textbooks. Cambridge University Press, 2013.
- Also names: [`data_assimilation`](data_assimilation.md)
- Registry: [EQ-FILT-BAYES-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-filt-bayes-001--sequential-bayesian-filtering-recursion)

### EQ-FILT-KF-001 — Kalman prediction (covariance propagation)

`m_k^- = F m_(k-1) + B u_k`, `P_k^- = F P_(k-1) F' + Q`.

### EQ-FILT-KF-002 — Kalman measurement update

`v_k = y_k - H m_k^-`, `S_k = H P_k^- H' + R`, `K_k = P_k^- H' S_k^-1`,
`m_k = m_k^- + K_k v_k`.

- Domain: [EQ-FILT-SSM-001] with positive-definite `S_k`.
- Meaning: Gaussian conditioning on one new observation; the gain weighs the
  innovation by the ratio of prediction to innovation uncertainty. The gain
  is computed by Cholesky solves, never by explicit inversion.
- Code: `filtering.kalman_update`, `filtering.linear_gaussian_update`,
  `filtering.gain_from_cross_covariance`.

### EQ-FILT-KF-005 — Discrete Riccati steady state

`P^- = F (P^- - P^- H' (H P^- H' + R)^-1 H P^-) F' + Q`, iterated from

- Domain: time-invariant model satisfying the usual detectability and
  stabilizability conditions.
- Meaning: the gain and covariance a long-running filter settles to;
  non-convergence is reported, not raised.
- Code: `filtering.steady_state_kalman`.

### EQ-FILT-RTS-001 — Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother

`G_k = C_(k+1) (P_(k+1)^-)^-1` with `C_(k+1) = Cov(x_k, x_(k+1)|y_1:k)`
(`= P_k F'` for linear dynamics), `m_k^s = m_k + G_k (m_(k+1)^s - m_(k+1)^-)`,
`P_k^s = P_k + G_k (P_(k+1)^s - P_(k+1)^-) G_k'`.

- Domain: positive-definite predicted covariances.
- Meaning: the backward pass that yields `p(x_k|y_1:T)`; it uses future data
  and so never has larger marginal variance than the filter. The same
  recursion smooths EKF (Jacobian) and UKF (sigma-point) outputs.
- Code: `filtering.rts_smoother`.

### EQ-FILT-EM-001 — EM M-step for a linear-Gaussian model

With smoothed `m^s_k, P^s_k` and gains `G_k`:
`P^s_(k,k-1) = P^s_k G_(k-1)'`,
`Sigma = T^-1 sum_(k=1..T) (P^s_k + m^s_k m^s_k')`,
`Phi = T^-1 sum_(k=1..T) (P^s_(k-1) + m^s_(k-1) m^s_(k-1)')`,
`C = T^-1 sum_(k=1..T) (P^s_(k,k-1) + m^s_k m^s_(k-1)')`; over the `T_o`
observed steps `Sigma_o = T_o^-1 sum (P^s_k + m^s_k m^s_k')`,
`B = T_o^-1 sum y_k m^s_k'`, `D = T_o^-1 sum y_k y_k'`. Updates:
`F = C Phi^-1`, `Q = Sigma - C F' - F C' + F Phi F'`, `H = B Sigma_o^-1`,
`R = D - H B' - B H' + H Sigma_o H'`, `m_0 = m^s_0`, `P_0 = P^s_0`.

- Domain: [EQ-FILT-SSM-001] without controls; positive-definite `Phi` and
  `Sigma_o`.
- Meaning: each update maximizes the expected complete-data log-likelihood
  given the blocks held fixed, so the exact log-likelihood never decreases.
- Code: `filtering.linear_gaussian_em`, `filtering.smoothed_lag_one_covariances`.
- Source: [REF-SHUMWAY-STOFFER-1982] and [REF-SARKKA-2013, Chapter 12].
  - [REF-SHUMWAY-STOFFER-1982](../references.md#ref-shumway-stoffer-1982): R. H. Shumway and D. S. Stoffer. "An Approach to Time Series Smoothing and Forecasting Using the EM Algorithm." *Journal of Time Series Analysis* 3(4), 253-264, 1982. DOI: [10.1111/j.1467-9892.1982.tb00349.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9892.1982.tb00349.x).

### EQ-FILT-PMMH-001 — Particle marginal Metropolis-Hastings acceptance

`theta' = theta + L z`, `z ~ N(0, I)`; accept with probability
`min(1, p(theta') p_hat(y_1:T | theta') / (p(theta) p_hat(y_1:T | theta)))`,
where `p_hat(y_1:T | theta)` is the stored estimate of the current state.

- Domain: `p_hat` a non-negative unbiased particle estimate; symmetric
  proposal.
- Meaning: an exact Metropolis-Hastings chain on parameters and filter
  randomness whose `theta`-marginal is the exact posterior for any particle
  count; re-estimating the current state would break this.
- Code: `filtering.particle_marginal_metropolis_hastings`.
- Source: [REF-ANDRIEU-DOUCET-HOLENSTEIN-2010].

### EQ-FILT-PF-003 — Systematic resampling

With one `U ~ Uniform[0,1)`, ancestor `j` is the smallest `i` with
`sum_(l<=i) W^l > (U + j)/N`, `j = 0..N-1`.

- Domain: normalized weights.
- Meaning: each particle receives `floor(N W^i)` or `ceil(N W^i)` copies with
  expected count `N W^i`, with less resampling noise than multinomial draws.
- Code: `filtering.systematic_resample`.

## `probstat.filtering.particle_proposals`

### EQ-FILT-APF-001 — Auxiliary particle filter weights and likelihood

First stage `lambda^i ∝ W_(k-1)^i g(y_k | mu_k^i)` with
`mu_k^i = f(x_(k-1)^i)` and `g(y | x) = N(y; h(x), R)`; ancestors
`a_j ~ lambda`, `x_k^j = mu_k^(a_j) + w_k^j`; second stage
`w_k^j = g(y_k | x_k^j) / g(y_k | mu_k^(a_j))`, `W_k^j ∝ w_k^j`;
`p_hat(y_k | y_1:k-1) = [sum_i W_(k-1)^i g(y_k | mu_k^i)] (1/N) sum_j w_k^j`.

- Domain: [EQ-FILT-SSM-002]; normalized previous weights.
- Meaning: resampling looks ahead to `y_k` through the predictive mean; the
  product of the increments is an unbiased estimate of `p(y_1:T)`. The
  point-estimate first stage has light tails, so the second-stage weights can
  be variable when `R` is small relative to the predictive spread.
- Code: `filtering.auxiliary_particle_filter`.
- Source: [REF-PITT-SHEPHARD-1999] and [REF-DOUCET-JOHANSEN-2011, Section 4.2].
  - [REF-PITT-SHEPHARD-1999](../references.md#ref-pitt-shephard-1999): Michael K. Pitt and Neil Shephard. The auxiliary particle filter: previous particles are resampled with first-stage weights that look ahead to the next observation, and second-stage weights correct for the look-ahead. DOI: [10.1080/01621459.1999.10474153](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1999.10474153).
  - [REF-DOUCET-JOHANSEN-2011](../references.md#ref-doucet-johansen-2011): Arnaud Doucet and Adam M. Johansen. "A Tutorial on Particle Filtering and Smoothing: Fifteen Years Later." In D. Crisan and B. Rozovskii (eds.), *The Oxford Handbook of Nonlinear Filtering*. Oxford University Press, 2011.

### EQ-FILT-OPT-001 — Locally optimal proposal for a linear observation

For `y_k = H x_k + v_k`: `mu = f(x_(k-1))`, `S = H Q H' + R`,
`K = Q H' S^-1`, `p(x_k | x_(k-1), y_k) = N(mu + K (y_k - H mu), Sigma)`,
`Sigma = (I - K H) Q (I - K H)' + K R K'`; weight
`W_k^i ∝ W_(k-1)^i N(y_k; H mu^i, S)`;
`p_hat(y_k | y_1:k-1) = sum_i W_(k-1)^i N(y_k; H mu^i, S)`.

- Domain: additive Gaussian noise, linear `H`, positive-definite `S`.
- Meaning: the proposal that minimizes the variance of the weights given the
  past; the weight `p(y_k | x_(k-1))` does not depend on the new particle.
- Code: `filtering.optimal_proposal_particle_filter`.
- Source: [REF-DOUCET-GODSILL-ANDRIEU-2000] and
  [REF-DOUCET-JOHANSEN-2011, Section 4.1].

"On Sequential Monte Carlo Sampling Methods for Bayesian Filtering." *Statistics and Computing* 10(3), 197-208, 2000. The proposal `p(x_k | x_(k-1), y_k)` that minimizes the conditional variance of the importance weights, and its closed form for Gaussian models with a linear observation equation. DOI: [10.1023/A:1008935410038](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008935410038).

## `probstat.filtering.state_space`

### EQ-FILT-SSM-001 — Linear-Gaussian state-space model

`x_k = F x_(k-1) + B u_k + w_k`, `w_k ~ N(0,Q)`; `y_k = H x_k + v_k`,
`v_k ~ N(0,R)`; `x_0 ~ N(m_0,P_0)`, with `w`, `v`, and `x_0` mutually
independent.

- Domain: `Q` and `P_0` symmetric positive semidefinite, `R` symmetric
  positive definite, conformable `F`, `B`, `H`; observations `y_1..y_T`
  follow the transitions.
- Meaning: `Q` is process (dynamics) uncertainty that inflates predictions;
  `R` is measurement uncertainty that limits how far one observation can move
  the estimate.
- Code: `filtering.LinearGaussianStateSpace`, `filtering.simulate`.
- Source: [REF-KALMAN-1960] and [REF-SARKKA-2013, Chapter 4].
  - [REF-KALMAN-1960](../references.md#ref-kalman-1960): R. E. Kalman. "A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems." *Transactions of the ASME, Journal of Basic Engineering* 82(1), 35-45, 1960. DOI: [10.1115/1.3662552](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552).

### EQ-FILT-SSM-002 — Nonlinear additive-noise state-space model
