## `probstat.monte_carlo.limit_simulation`

### EQ-LIM-009 — Simulation estimates of convergence

There are three estimators:

- weak law: `p_hat_n = R^-1 sum_r 1{|Xbar_n^(r) - mu| > eps}`, with standard
  error `sqrt(p_hat (1 - p_hat)/R)`;
- strong law, finite horizon: `R^-1 sum_r 1{max_{m <= k <= N}
  |Xbar_k^(r) - mu| > eps}`;
- distribution: `D_R = sup_z |F_R(z) - Phi(z)|` for `R` simulated
  standardized means.

- Domain: `R` independent replications on child streams.
- Meaning: illustrations with Monte Carlo error. `D_R` adds noise of order
  `R^-1/2` (DKW) to the true Kolmogorov distance. The finite-horizon
  frequency lower-bounds the infinite-horizon probability.
- Code: `monte_carlo.simulate_weak_law`,
  `monte_carlo.simulate_running_means`,
  `monte_carlo.simulate_clt_distance`.
- Source: [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002], [REF-WASSERMAN-2004],

## `probstat.monte_carlo.multilevel`

### EQ-MC-MLMC-001 — Multilevel telescoping estimator

`E[P_L] = E[P_0] + sum_(l=1..L) E[P_l - P_(l-1)]`, estimated by
`Y_hat = sum_(l=0..L) (1/N_l) sum_(n=1..N_l) (P_l^(n,l) - P_(l-1)^(n,l))` with
`P_(-1) = 0`; `Var(Y_hat) = sum_l V_l / N_l`, `V_l = Var(P_l - P_(l-1))`.

- Domain: square-integrable approximations; the fine and coarse values of
  one sample `(n, l)` share randomness, different levels are independent.
- Meaning: the bias is that of the finest level `L`, while most samples are
  spent on cheap coarse levels because coupling makes `V_l` small.
- Code: `monte_carlo.multilevel_monte_carlo`,
  `monte_carlo.adaptive_multilevel_monte_carlo`,
  `monte_carlo.MultilevelResult.standard_error`.
- Source: [REF-GILES-2008] and [REF-GILES-2015].
  - [REF-GILES-2008](../references.md#ref-giles-2008): Michael B. Giles. "Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation." *Operations Research* 56(3), 607-617, 2008.
  - [REF-GILES-2015](../references.md#ref-giles-2015): Michael B. Giles. "Multilevel Monte Carlo Methods." *Acta Numerica* 24, 259-328, 2015.

### EQ-MC-MLMC-002 — Cost-optimal multilevel allocation

Minimizing `sum_l N_l C_l` subject to `sum_l V_l / N_l = v` gives
`N_l = ceil(sqrt(V_l / C_l) sum_k sqrt(V_k C_k) / v)` and total cost
`(sum_k sqrt(V_k C_k))^2 / v`. A coupled sample costs
`C_l = c(l) + c(l-1)` (`C_0 = c(0)`) for fine-path cost `c(l)`; plain Monte
Carlo on level `L` with the same variance costs `Var(P_L) c(L) / v`.

- Domain: `V_l >= 0`, `C_l > 0`, `v > 0`.
- Meaning: Lagrange-multiplier allocation; samples go where variance per
  unit cost is largest.
- Code: `monte_carlo.optimal_level_allocation`,
  `monte_carlo.MultilevelResult.single_level_cost`,
  `monte_carlo.MultilevelResult.cost_ratio`.
- Source: [REF-GILES-2008] and [REF-GILES-2015].
  - [REF-GILES-2008](../references.md#ref-giles-2008): Michael B. Giles. "Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation." *Operations Research* 56(3), 607-617, 2008.

### EQ-MC-MLMC-003 — Rate regression, bias extrapolation, and MSE split

`|E[Y_l]| ~ M^(-alpha l)`, `V_l ~ M^(-beta l)`, `C_l ~ M^(gamma l)`, fitted by
least squares of `log_M` values on `l >= 1`. Remaining bias
`|E[P - P_L]| ~ max_(j=0,1,2) m_(L-j) M^(-j alpha) / (M^alpha - 1)`. With
`Var(Y_hat) <= eps^2/2` and bias `<= eps/sqrt 2`,
`MSE = Var(Y_hat) + bias^2 <= eps^2`.

- Domain: geometric refinement factor `M >= 2`; `alpha > 0` for the
  extrapolation (the adaptive driver uses `max(alpha, 1/2)`).
- Meaning: Giles' complexity theorem gives total cost `O(eps^-2)` when
  `beta > gamma`, `O(eps^-2 (log eps)^2)` when `beta = gamma`, and
  `O(eps^(-2-(gamma-beta)/alpha))` when `beta < gamma`; the adaptive driver
  adds levels until the extrapolated bias passes.
- Code: `monte_carlo.convergence_rates`,
  `monte_carlo.adaptive_multilevel_monte_carlo`,
  `monte_carlo.MultilevelResult.rmse_estimate`.
- Source: [REF-GILES-2008] and [REF-GILES-2015].
  - [REF-GILES-2008](../references.md#ref-giles-2008): Michael B. Giles. "Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation." *Operations Research* 56(3), 607-617, 2008.
  - [REF-GILES-2015](../references.md#ref-giles-2015): Michael B. Giles. "Multilevel Monte Carlo Methods." *Acta Numerica* 24, 259-328, 2015.

## `probstat.monte_carlo.stein`

### EQ-MC-STEIN-001 — Langevin-Stein operator and polynomial zero-variance controls

`(A phi)(x) = Laplacian phi(x) + grad phi(x) . u(x)` with
`u = grad log pi`; for `phi = x_j`: `A phi = u_j`; for `phi = x_j^2`:
`A phi = 2 + 2 x_j u_j`; for `phi = x_j x_k` (`j < k`):
`A phi = x_k u_j + x_j u_k`. Degree 1 gives `p = d` controls and degree 2
gives `p = d + d(d+1)/2`.

- Domain: `pi` continuously differentiable and positive, `phi pi` and
  `grad phi pi` vanishing at infinity so that integration by parts gives
  `E_pi[A phi] = 0`; only the score is needed, not the normalizing constant.
- Meaning: zero-mean controls built from the target alone. Mira, Solgi and
  Imparato write the renormalized integrand as
  `f - (1/2) Laplacian P + grad P . z` with `z = -(1/2) grad log pi`, which is
  `f - (1/2) A P`; the factor `-1/2` is absorbed into the fitted
  coefficients. For a Gaussian target the degree-2 controls span every
  centred polynomial of degree at most two, so such integrands are
  integrated exactly.
- Code: `monte_carlo.stein_polynomial_controls`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_zero_variance`.
- Source: [REF-ASSARAF-CAFFAREL-1999], [REF-MIRA-SOLGI-IMPARATO-2013,

### EQ-MC-STEIN-002 — Least-squares zero-variance estimator and variance-reduction factor

`(b, theta) = argmin sum_i (f_i - b - theta' h_i)^2 + lambda ||theta||^2`,
`mu_hat = n^-1 sum_i (f_i - theta' h_i)`,
`s_adj^2 = sum_i (c_i - mu_hat)^2 / (n - p - 1)` with
`c_i = f_i - theta' h_i`, `SE = sqrt(s_adj^2 / n)`, and
`VRF = s_f^2 / s_adj^2`.

- Domain: `n >= p + 2` points and a full-rank design `[1, H]` when
  `lambda = 0`; `lambda >= 0` penalizes `theta` only.
- Meaning: the ordinary least-squares `theta` equals the sample version of
  `Cov(h)^-1 Cov(h, f)`, the multivariate form of the optimal coefficient
  `a = -Sigma_zz^-1 sigma(z, f)` of the source. Fitting on the same sample
  introduces an `O(1/n)` bias, and the reported standard error is a plug-in
  value. With `standard_error="mcmc"` the IID formula is replaced by the
  effective-sample-size standard error of the contribution series
  [EQ-MCMC-MCSE-001], scaled by `sqrt(s_adj^2 / s_c^2)`.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_zero_variance(method="same-sample")`.
- Source: [REF-MIRA-SOLGI-IMPARATO-2013, Sections 4 and 5],
  [REF-GOLUB-VANLOAN-2013, Section 5.3].
  - [REF-MIRA-SOLGI-IMPARATO-2013](../references.md#ref-mira-solgi-imparato-2013): Antonietta Mira, Reza Solgi, and Daniele Imparato. "Zero Variance Markov Chain Monte Carlo for Bayesian Estimators." *Statistics and Computing* 23(5), 653-662, 2013. doi:10.1007/s11222-012-9344-6. Section and equation numbers follow arXiv:1012.2983.

### EQ-MC-STEIN-003 — Cross-fitted (split-sample) Stein estimator

Indices are cut into `K` contiguous folds `F_1, ..., F_K`; for `i` in `F_k`,
`c_i = f_i - g_(-k)(x_i)`, where `g_(-k)` is fitted without `F_k`
(`theta_(-k)' h` for zero-variance controls, `psi_(-k)` for control
functionals); `mu_hat = n^-1 sum_i c_i` and `SE = sqrt(s_c^2 / n)`.

- Domain: independent draws for exact unbiasedness, `K >= 2`, and enough
  training points per fold for the fit.
- Meaning: since `g_(-k)` has zero mean under `pi` and is independent of the
  held-out point, `E[c_i] = E_pi[f]` exactly. With `K = 2` it is the
  symmetrized form of the source's split estimator on `D_0` and `D_1`. The
  standard error ignores the weak dependence between folds through the
  fitted functions. For Markov-chain input the contiguous folds keep
  training and held-out blocks nearly independent, but unbiasedness then
  holds only asymptotically.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_zero_variance(method="split-sample")`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional`.

### EQ-MC-STEIN-004 — Stein reproducing kernel of a radial base kernel

`k0(x, y) = div_x div_y k + u(x) . grad_y k + u(y) . grad_x k +
u(x) . u(y) k`. For `k(x, y) = g(z)`, `z = ||x - y||^2`, `r = x - y`:
`k0 = -4 g''(z) z - 2 d g'(z) - 2 g'(z) (u(x) - u(y)) . r + (u(x) . u(y)) g(z)`;
Gaussian `g(z) = exp(-z / (2 l^2))`; inverse multiquadric
`g(z) = (c^2 + z)^beta`, `c > 0`, `beta` in `(-1, 0)`.

- Domain: twice continuously differentiable base kernel and a score `u`;
  `E_pi[k0(X, y)] = 0` for every `y` under the integration-by-parts
  conditions of the sources.
- Meaning: `k0` is the reproducing kernel of the image of the base-kernel
  Hilbert space under the Langevin-Stein operator, so every function
  `sum_j a_j k0(., x_j)` is a zero-mean control functional. The radial
  expansion follows from `grad_x k = 2 g' r`, `grad_y k = -2 g' r` and
  `div_x div_y k = -4 g'' z - 2 d g'`.

### EQ-MC-STEIN-005 — Control-functional fit and kernel-quadrature weights

`A = K0 + lambda mean(diag K0) I`, `c = 1' A^-1 f / 1' A^-1 1`,
`a = A^-1 (f - c 1)`, `psi(x) = sum_j a_j k0(x, x_j)`; on all `n` points the
estimate is `c = w' f` with `w = A^-1 1 / (1' A^-1 1)`, `sum_i w_i = 1`.

- Domain: `K0` from [EQ-MC-STEIN-004]; `lambda` starts at the requested
  relative value (default `1e-8`) and is raised by powers of ten until the
  Cholesky factorization of `A` succeeds, up to `1e-2`.
- Meaning: least-squares fit of `f` by a constant plus a control functional;
  `psi` evaluated on held-out points gives the split estimator
  [EQ-MC-STEIN-003], and the constant fitted on all points is the
  simplified estimator, a quadrature rule with possibly negative weights. The
  source picks the smallest power of ten giving condition number below
  `1e10`; the implementation uses Cholesky success instead, which avoids an
  `O(n^3)` condition estimate.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional_weights`.

### EQ-MC-STEIN-006 — Length-scale selection by median heuristic and cross-validation

`l_med = sqrt(H_n / 2)`, `H_n = median_(i<j) ||x_i - x_j||^2`; the
cross-validated choice minimizes
`sum_k sum_(i in F_k) (f_i - c_(-k) - psi_(-k)(x_i))^2` over the grid
`l_med * {1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4}` with five contiguous folds of the training
points.

- Domain: at least two distinct points; selection uses only the training
  points of the fit it serves, so the split estimator stays unbiased.
- Meaning: the median heuristic fixes the scale of the grid;
  cross-validation, used by the source for the kernel hyperparameters,
  chooses within it. For an `N(0, 1)` target and `f = cos`, the selected
  value was four times the median heuristic in most seeds.
- Code: `monte_carlo.median_heuristic_length_scale`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional(length_scale="cv")`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional_weights(length_scale="cv")`.
- Source: [REF-GARREAU-JITKRITTUM-KANAGAWA-2017, Section 2.2, Eq. (2)],
  [REF-OATES-GIROLAMI-CHOPIN-2017, Section 2.3.4].

### EQ-MC-STEIN-007 — Kernel Stein discrepancy V- and U-statistics

`KSD^2_V = n^-2 sum_(i,j) k0(x_i, x_j)`, `KSD = sqrt(KSD^2_V)`,
`KSD^2_U = (n(n-1))^-1 sum_(i != j) k0(x_i, x_j)`; for a weighted sample,
`KSD_w = sqrt(w' K0 w)`.

- Domain: [EQ-MC-STEIN-004] with the default inverse multiquadric kernel
  `c = 1`, `beta = -1/2`; samples from any source.
- Meaning: the V-statistic is the squared discrepancy of the empirical
  measure, the supremum of `|E_Q[div g + g . u]|` over vector fields `g` in
  the unit ball of the `d`-fold product of the base Hilbert space; the U-statistic is unbiased for independent draws and may
  be negative. With `beta` in `(-1, 0)` the IMQ discrepancy detects
  non-convergence for distantly dissipative targets. `KSD_w` is the
  worst-case integration error of the control-functional weights.
- Code: `monte_carlo.kernel_stein_discrepancy`,
  `monte_carlo.estimate_control_functional_weights`.
- Source: [REF-GORHAM-MACKEY-2017, Proposition 2 and Theorem 8],
  [REF-LIU-LEE-JORDAN-2016, Theorem 3.6, Eq. (9), and Section 4, Eq. (14)].
  - [REF-GORHAM-MACKEY-2017](../references.md#ref-gorham-mackey-2017): Jackson Gorham and Lester Mackey. "Measuring Sample Quality with Kernels." In *Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning*, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 70, 1292-1301, 2017. Section, proposition, and theorem numbers follow arXiv:1703.01717.
  - [REF-LIU-LEE-JORDAN-2016](../references.md#ref-liu-lee-jordan-2016): Qiang Liu, Jason D. Lee, and Michael I. Jordan. "A Kernelized Stein Discrepancy for Goodness-of-fit Tests." In *Proceedings of the 33rd International Conference on Machine Learning*, Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 48, 276-284, 2016. Theorem and equation numbers follow the extended version, arXiv:1602.03253v2.

## `probstat.monte_carlo.variance_reduction`

### EQ-MC-ANTI-001 — Antithetic-pair estimator

`Z_i=[f(U_i)+f(1-U_i)]/2` and `mu_hat_A=(1/m) sum_i Z_i`, where
`U_i~Uniform(0,1)` independently across pairs.

- Domain: integrable unit-interval `f`, at least two independent pairs, and
  finite evaluated values; gains require favorable negative covariance within
  a pair and are not guaranteed for arbitrary functions.
- Meaning: preserves `E[f(U)]`, charges two function evaluations per independent
  pair, and estimates uncertainty from the pair averages rather than treating
  the negatively dependent evaluations as independent.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_antithetic_uniform`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 8.2].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-ANTI-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-anti-001--antithetic-pair-estimator)

### EQ-MC-CONTROL-001 — Control-variate adjustment

`Y_i^(cv)=Y_i-beta(C_i-mu_C)` with
`beta*=Cov(Y,C)/Var(C)` when `Var(C)>0`.

- Domain: paired finite target/control observations and a genuinely known
  control mean `mu_C`; the automatic plug-in coefficient also requires
  positive sample control variance.
- Meaning: the adjustment preserves the target expectation for a fixed
  coefficient and can reduce variance when the control is correlated with the
  target. A coefficient fitted on the same sample makes the reported standard
  error a plug-in diagnostic rather than an exact finite-sample result.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_control_variate`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 8.9].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-CONTROL-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-control-001--control-variate-adjustment)

### EQ-MC-COND-001 — Conditional Monte Carlo identity

`E[Y]=E[E[Y|Z]]` and
`Var(Y)=E[Var(Y|Z)]+Var(E[Y|Z])`.

- Domain: integrable `Y`; the variance decomposition requires finite second
  moments and an actual conditional expectation under the intended model.
- Meaning: sampling `E[Y|Z]` instead of raw `Y` preserves the expectation and
  cannot have larger population variance.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_conditional_expectation`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 8.1].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-COND-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-cond-001--conditional-monte-carlo-identity)

### EQ-MC-STRAT-001 — Stratified mean and variance estimator

`mu_hat_st=sum_h W_h mu_hat_h` and
`Var_hat(mu_hat_st)=sum_h W_h^2 s_h^2/n_h`.

- Domain: disjoint exhaustive strata with known weights `W_h`, independent
  within-stratum samples, and at least two samples per stratum for each `s_h^2`.
  The implementation uses equal-width unit-interval strata, so `W_h=1/H`.
- Meaning: combines local means and accounts for heterogeneous allocation
  without pretending all sampled outputs are identically distributed.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_stratified_uniform`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 8.4].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-STRAT-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-strat-001--stratified-mean-and-variance-estimator)

### EQ-MC-IS-001 — Ordinary importance-sampling identity

`E_p[g(X)]=E_q[g(X) w(X)]`, where `w(x)=p(x)/q(x)`.

- Domain: the proposal `q` is positive wherever `|g|p` contributes, the target
  and proposal densities are evaluated consistently, and the weighted
  integrand has the moments needed for the requested error summary.
- Meaning: draws come from a convenient proposal while density-ratio weights
  preserve the target expectation. The implementation is ordinary, not
  self-normalized, importance sampling.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_importance_sampling`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Chapter 9].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-IS-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-is-001--ordinary-importance-sampling-identity)

### EQ-MC-ESS-001 — Weight-only effective sample size diagnostic

`ESS_w=(sum_i w_i)^2/(sum_i w_i^2)`.

- Domain: non-negative finite weights with at least one positive weight.
- Meaning: reports weight concentration on a scale from one to `N`; it is a
  heuristic diagnostic and is not the estimator's actual independent-sample
  count or a replacement for its standard error.
- Code: `monte_carlo.estimate_importance_sampling`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 9.3].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-ESS-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-ess-001--weight-only-effective-sample-size-diagnostic)

### EQ-MC-NEYMAN-001 — Stratified simulation-budget allocation

`n_h proportional to W_h sigma_h` for equal per-sample costs.

- Domain: fixed total budget, known or pilot-estimated non-negative stratum
  standard deviations, and the classical independent stratified estimator.
- Meaning: the unconstrained Neyman rule minimizes stratified estimator
  variance. The implementation first enforces an integer minimum in every
  equal-width stratum, then apportions the remaining budget proportionally by
  largest remainders; this lower-bounded construction is transparent and
  deterministic but need not equal the exact constrained optimum.
- Code: `monte_carlo.allocate_stratified_budget`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Section 8.4].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
- Registry: [EQ-MC-NEYMAN-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-mc-neyman-001--stratified-simulation-budget-allocation)

### EQ-MC-EFF-001 — Cost-normalized estimator efficiency

`score=SE(mu_hat)^2 C` and
`gain_(base->cand)=score_base/score_candidate`, where `C` is charged work.

- Domain: comparable estimands, non-negative finite estimated standard errors,
  and positive evaluation counts; both-zero scores are undefined.
- Meaning: under the usual inverse-cost variance scaling, a gain above one
  means the candidate delivers more precision per evaluation. It is an
  empirical comparison, not a universal ranking independent of model and
  integrand.
- Code: `monte_carlo.compare_efficiency` and
  `visualization.plot_efficiency_comparison`.
- Source: [REF-OWEN-2013, Chapters 2 and 8].
  - [REF-OWEN-2013](../references.md#ref-owen-2013): Art B. Owen. *Monte Carlo Theory, Methods and Examples*. 2013. [https://artowen.su.domains/mc/](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/).
