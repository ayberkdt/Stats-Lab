### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-001 — Law of propagation of uncertainty with correlation

`u_c^2(y) = sum_i sum_j c_i c_j u(x_i) u(x_j) r(x_i, x_j)` with
`c_i = df/dx_i` at the input estimates and `r_ii = 1`; for independent inputs
`u_c^2 = sum_i (c_i u_i)^2`. For a budget with a correlation matrix `R` this
is `(c * u)' R (c * u)`.

- Domain: finite `c_i`, `u_i >= 0`, symmetric positive-semidefinite `R` with
  unit diagonal; first-order Taylor expansion of `f`, exact for linear `f`.
- Meaning: the standard uncertainty of the output of a model when the
  uncertainties of its inputs are known; the model is linearised at the
  estimates, so curvature is ignored (see EQ-UQ-BUDGET-006).
- Code: `uq.UncertaintyBudget.combined_variance`,
  `uq.UncertaintyBudget.combined_standard_uncertainty`,
  `uq.UncertaintyBudget.law_of_propagation`, `uq.budget_from_model`,
  `uq.BudgetEntry`.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-002 — Welch-Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom and expanded uncertainty

`nu_eff = u_c^4 / sum_i (c_i u_i)^4 / nu_i`, `k = t_((1+p)/2)(nu_eff)`,
`U = k u_c`; infinite `nu_i` contribute nothing to the sum and the normal
quantile replaces the t quantile when `nu_eff` is infinite.

- Domain: uncorrelated inputs. With a correlation matrix the code evaluates
  the same expression with the correlated `u_c` in the numerator, a heuristic
  that the result flags in its warnings.
- Meaning: approximates the law of `(y - Y) / u_c` by a Student-t law so that
  `y +/- U` has approximately coverage `p`.
- Code: `uq.UncertaintyBudget.effective_degrees_of_freedom`,

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-003 — Standard uncertainty of a Type A or Type B evaluation

Rectangular on `[-a, a]`: `u = a / sqrt(3)`; symmetric triangular:
`u = a / sqrt(6)`; arcsine (U-shaped): `u = a / sqrt(2)`; quoted expanded
uncertainty `U` with coverage factor `k`: `u = U / k`; any law: `u` is its
standard deviation. Type A from `n` readings: `u = s / sqrt(n)` with
`s^2 = sum (q_j - mean)^2 / (n - 1)` and `nu = n - 1`.

- Domain: `a >= 0`, `k > 0`, `n >= 2`, finite standard deviation.
- Meaning: converts the information about an input (limits, a certificate, a
  distribution, repeated readings) into a standard uncertainty.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-004 — Combination rules: root-sum-square, linear sum, mixed

Root-sum-square: `sqrt(sum (c_i u_i)^2)`. Linear sum of absolute
contributions: `sum |c_i| a_i` over half-widths `a_i` (a bound) or
`sum |c_i| u_i` over standard uncertainties (the largest `u_c` under any
correlation, by the triangle inequality). Mixed rule at standard level:
`sum_(bias) |c_i| u_i + sqrt(sum_(random) (c_j u_j)^2)`; at bound level:
`sum_(bias) |c_i| a_i + k sqrt(sum_(random) (c_j u_j)^2)` with `k` stated.

- Domain: finite `c_i`, `u_i >= 0`, `a_i >= 0`, `k > 0`; the bound forms need
  a half-width for every (bias) entry.
- Meaning: conventions of system engineering for adding budget terms. Each
  result carries its kind (standard uncertainty or worst-case bound) so that
  values of different kinds are not combined silently. The mixed rule is a
  convention, not a result of the Guide.
- Code: `uq.UncertaintyBudget.root_sum_square`,
  `uq.UncertaintyBudget.worst_case_sum`, `uq.UncertaintyBudget.mixed_rule`,
  `uq.CombinedUncertainty`, `uq.QuantityKind`.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-005 — Variance share of a budget entry

`share_i = c_i u_i (sum_j r_ij c_j u_j) / u_c^2`; the shares add to one,
reduce to `(c_i u_i)^2 / u_c^2` for independent inputs, and can be negative or
above one under correlation.

- Domain: `u_c > 0`; undefined (`nan`) when `u_c = 0`.
- Meaning: apportions the combined variance to the entries by splitting each
  covariance term equally between its two inputs.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-006 — Second-order check of the linearisation

For Gaussian inputs with covariance `S` and Hessian `H` of the model:
mean shift `tr(H S) / 2`, extra variance `tr(H S H S) / 2`, linear variance
`c' S c`; ratios `|shift| / u_c` and `sqrt(1 + extra / u_c^2) - 1` are
compared with a tolerance.

- Domain: Gaussian input approximation, twice-differentiable model; the
  third-derivative terms of the full second-order propagation are not
  included.
- Meaning: diagnoses a poor first-order propagation (curvature matters
  relative to the input spread); it does not replace the Monte Carlo
  validation of EQ-UQ-BUDGET-009.
- Code: `uq.budget_from_model`, `uq.LinearizationCheck`, `uq.ModelBudget`.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-007 — Monte Carlo estimate, standard uncertainty and coverage intervals

`y = (1/M) sum y_r`, `u^2(y) = sum (y_r - y)^2 / (M - 1)`. With sorted values
`y_(1) <= ... <= y_(M)`, `q = pM` (integer part of `pM + 1/2` otherwise), the
interval `[y_(r), y_(r+q)]` is a `100p %` coverage interval; symmetric:
`r = (M - q)/2` (integer part of `(M - q + 1)/2` otherwise); shortest: the
`r` minimising `y_(r+q) - y_(r)`. Monte Carlo errors: `u(y)/sqrt(M)` for `y`,
`u(y) sqrt((kurtosis - 1)/(4M))` for `u(y)`, and
`sqrt(a(1-a)/M) / g` for an endpoint at level `a` with the output density `g`
estimated from the spacing of order statistics.

- Domain: `M >= 20`, `0 < p < 1`, `M - q >= 1`, finite model values; the
  moments must exist.
- Meaning: summarises the discrete representation of the output distribution
  obtained by propagating the input distributions through the model.
- Code: `uq.monte_carlo_budget`, `uq.MonteCarloResult`,

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-008 — Gaussian copula of correlated inputs

`z = L e` with `L L' = R` and `e ~ N(0, I)`, `u_i = Phi(z_i)`,
`x_i = F_i^(-1)(u_i)`. Marginals are the stated laws `F_i`; the dependence is
that of a Gaussian vector with correlation `R` on the normal scores. For
normal margins `R` is the Pearson correlation; the Spearman correlation of a
pair is `(6/pi) asin(r/2)`.

- Domain: `R` symmetric positive semidefinite with unit diagonal; margins
  with a quantile function.
- Meaning: one way to generate dependent inputs with prescribed margins; a
  modelling choice, not implied by a correlation coefficient alone.

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-009 — Validation of the GUM uncertainty framework by Monte Carlo

Numerical tolerance: write `u(y) = c x 10^l` with `c` an integer of `ndig`
decimal digits, then `delta = 10^l / 2`. The GUM interval is `y +/- U_p`;
`d_low = |y - U_p - y_low|`, `d_high = |y + U_p - y_high|`; the framework is
validated for the stated `p` if both are at most `delta`.

- Domain: `u(y) > 0`, `ndig >= 1`; holds for the stated coverage probability
  only.
- Meaning: compares the interval of the law of propagation with a Monte
  Carlo interval to the number of significant digits wanted in `u(y)`. For an
  adaptive Monte Carlo result (EQ-UQ-BUDGET-010) the flag
  `monte_carlo_adequate` is the Supplement's own test, twice each
  stabilisation deviation at most `delta / 5` (Section 8.2); for a result of
  fixed size it is a proxy built from the Monte Carlo standard errors.
- Code: `uq.compare_with_law_of_propagation`, `uq.numerical_tolerance`,

### EQ-UQ-BUDGET-010 — Adaptive Monte Carlo procedure and its stopping rule

Run batches of `M = max(ceil(100 / (1 - p)), 10^4)` trials; batch `r` gives
`y^(r)`, `u(y^(r))` and the endpoints `y_low^(r)`, `y_high^(r)` of a `100p %`
coverage interval. After `h >= 2` batches, for each of the four per-batch
quantities `v^(r)`: `s_v^2 = sum_r (v^(r) - vbar)^2 / (h (h - 1))`. With
`u(y)` from all `h M` values written `c x 10^l` (`c` an integer of `ndig`
digits), `delta = 10^l / 2`. The computation has stabilised when
`2 s_y`, `2 s_u(y)`, `2 s_ylow` and `2 s_yhigh` are all at most `delta`
(it continues if any *exceeds* `delta`); the result is then computed from all
`h M` values. Validating the GUM framework uses `delta / 5` (Section 8.2).

- Domain: `0 < p < 1`, `ndig >= 1`, finite model values with `u(y) > 0`,
  `M >= 20`. The clause gives no probability that the stabilised numbers meet
  `delta` (Section 7.9.3, Note 1); when the batch limit is reached first the
  record says `stabilized = False`.
- Meaning: the sample standard deviation of the average of `h` independent
  batch values, used as the Monte Carlo error of each of the four reported
  numbers; the factor 2 is a Gaussian 95 % rule (Note 6). Stopping at the
  earliest `h` at which the test passes makes the realised error somewhat
  larger than the nominal one.

  `uq.average_standard_deviation`, `uq.adaptive_stopping_rule`,
  `uq.budget._clause_batch_size`.

### EQ-UQ-001 — Named forward-model map

`Y = g(X)`, with requested components `Y_q = g_q(X)` for quantities of

- Domain: `X` is a finite named parameter realization, `g` is deterministic

  contract; the quantity of interest determines which model response is used.

### EQ-VV-001 — Observed order under uniform refinement

`p = log(|phi_coarse - phi_medium| / |phi_medium - phi_fine|) / log(r)`.

- Domain: three finite solutions from uniform refinement ratio `r>1`, non-zero
  consecutive differences, and an asymptotic single-term error regime.
- Meaning: estimates how rapidly the selected quantity converges as resolution
  is refined; a value alone does not prove asymptotic convergence.
- Code: `uq.verification.observed_order_of_accuracy`.

### EQ-VV-002 — Richardson extrapolation

`phi_ext = phi_fine + (phi_fine - phi_medium)/(r^p - 1)`.

- Domain: finite medium and fine solutions, uniform `r>1`, positive order
  `p`, and the same asymptotic leading-error assumption as [EQ-VV-001].
- Meaning: cancels the estimated leading discretization-error term to predict

### EQ-VV-GCI-002 — Three-grid observed order with non-uniform ratios

Grids fine (1), medium (2), coarse (3): `r21 = h2/h1`, `r32 = h3/h2`,
`eps21 = phi2 - phi1`, `eps32 = phi3 - phi2`, `s = sign(eps32/eps21)`,
`p = |ln|eps32/eps21| + q(p)| / ln r21`,
`q(p) = ln((r21^p - s)/(r32^p - s))`, solved by fixed-point iteration from
`q = 0`. For monotone data the equivalent strictly increasing equation
`eps32/eps21 = r21^p (r32^p - 1)/(r21^p - 1)` is solved by Brent's method when
the iteration fails.

- Domain: `h1 < h2 < h3`, non-zero differences; for a uniform ratio `q = 0`
  and the formula reduces to [EQ-VV-001].
- Meaning: the rate at which the QoI converges, estimated from three grids
  without requiring a constant refinement ratio.
- Code: `uq.verification._observed_order`,
  `uq.verification._monotone_order_equation`,
  `uq.verification.grid_convergence_index`.

### EQ-VV-GCI-003 — Extrapolated value and relative errors

`phi_ext^21 = (r21^p phi1 - phi2)/(r21^p - 1) = phi1 + (phi1 - phi2)/(r21^p - 1)`,
`e_a^21 = |(phi1 - phi2)/phi1|`, `e_ext^21 = |(phi_ext^21 - phi1)/phi_ext^21|`.

- Domain: monotone asymptotic convergence with order `p > 0`; relative errors
  are undefined (reported as `None`) when the denominator is zero.
- Meaning: Richardson extrapolation with the observed order and the size of
  the last refinement step relative to the solution.
- Code: `uq.verification.grid_convergence_index`,
  `uq.verification.GridConvergenceResult`.

### EQ-VV-GCI-004 — Fine-grid Grid Convergence Index

Absolute: `GCI_fine^21 = F_s |phi1 - phi2| / (r21^p - 1)`; relative:
`GCI_fine,rel^21 = F_s e_a^21 / (r21^p - 1)`; coarse pair:
`GCI^32 = F_s |phi2 - phi3| / (r32^p - 1)`. `F_s = 1.25` for three or more
grids with an observed order; `F_s = 3` when the order is not trusted.

- Domain: as [EQ-VV-GCI-003].
- Meaning: an error band `phi1 +/- GCI` intended to bracket the
  discretization error with high (about 95 %) confidence; with the exact order
  it equals `F_s` times the true fine-grid error.
- Code: `uq.verification.grid_convergence_index`,
  `uq.verification.verify_solution`.
- Source: [REF-ROACHE-1998, Chapters 4-5], [REF-CELIK-ETAL-2008].

### EQ-VV-GCI-005 — Asymptotic-range check

`GCI^32 / (r21^p GCI^21) ~ 1`. With absolute GCIs at a reference order `p`
the ratio equals `(eps32/eps21) / f(p)`, `f(p) = r21^p (r32^p - 1)/(r21^p - 1)`;
with relative GCIs (Roache's form, `GCI^32` relative to `phi2`) it is
additionally multiplied by `|phi1/phi2|`.

- Domain: monotone three-grid data; reference order positive.
- Meaning: whether the grids behave as a single leading error term predicts.
  At the observed order of the same grids the absolute ratio is identically
  one (an algebraic consequence of [EQ-VV-GCI-002]); evaluated at the
  theoretical order it tests the asymptotic range (for a uniform ratio it
  equals `r^(p_obs - p_th)`).
- Code: `uq.verification.GridConvergenceResult.asymptotic_ratio_at`,
  `uq.verification.GridConvergenceResult.in_asymptotic_range`,
  `uq.verification.grid_convergence_index`, `uq.verification.verify_solution`.
- Source: [REF-ROACHE-1998, Chapters 4-5], [REF-CELIK-ETAL-2008].
  - [REF-ROACHE-1998](../references.md#ref-roache-1998): Patrick J. Roache. *Verification and Validation in Computational Science and Engineering*. Hermosa Publishers, 1998. ISBN 978-0-913478-08-0.
  - [REF-CELIK-ETAL-2008](../references.md#ref-celik-etal-2008): Ismail B. Celik, Urmila Ghia, Patrick J. Roache, Christopher J. Freitas, Hugh Coleman, and Peter E. Raad. ### EQ-VV-GCI-006 — Convergence classification

`R = eps32 / eps21`: oscillatory if `R < 0`; monotone if
`R > ln r32 / ln r21` (the `p -> 0` limit of `f(p)`, which is one for a
uniform ratio); otherwise divergent.

- Domain: non-zero differences.
- Meaning: Richardson-based estimates require monotone convergence;
  oscillatory sequences are flagged and divergent ones admit no positive
  order. With the fine-over-coarse ratio `eps21/eps32` used by Roy (2005), a
  uniform ratio gives monotone convergence for values in `(0, 1)`.
- Code: `uq.verification.classify_convergence`, `uq.verification._classify`,
  `uq.verification.ConvergenceType`.
- Source: [REF-CELIK-ETAL-2008], [REF-ROY-2005].
  - [REF-CELIK-ETAL-2008](../references.md#ref-celik-etal-2008): Ismail B. Celik, Urmila Ghia, Patrick J. Roache, Christopher J. Freitas, Hugh Coleman, and Peter E. Raad. ### EQ-VV-LSQ-001 — Least-squares observed order

`S(phi_0, alpha, p) = sum_i (phi_i - phi_0 - alpha (h_i/h_1)^p)^2`; for fixed
`p` the minimizer in `(phi_0, alpha)` is ordinary linear regression on
`x_i = (h_i/h_1)^p`; `p` minimizes the profile `S(p)` (400-point log scan plus
golden-section refinement); `U_s = sqrt(S_min/(n - 3))`.

- Domain: `n >= 4` distinct spacings, non-constant data, `p` inside declared
  bounds.
- Meaning: with more grids than unknowns the order is fitted rather than
  solved, so scatter in the data is visible in `U_s` instead of corrupting
  `p`; `alpha` is the fitted error of the finest solution.
- Code: `uq.verification.least_squares_convergence`,
  `uq.verification._fixed_order_fit`.
- Source: [REF-ECA-HOEKSTRA-2014], [REF-BRENT-1973, Chapter 5].
  - [REF-ECA-HOEKSTRA-2014](../references.md#ref-eca-hoekstra-2014): L. Eça and M. Hoekstra. "A Procedure for the Estimation of the Numerical Uncertainty of CFD Calculations Based on Grid Refinement Studies." *Journal of Computational Physics* 262, 104-130, 2014.
  - [REF-BRENT-1973](../references.md#ref-brent-1973): Richard P. Brent. *Algorithms for Minimization without Derivatives*. Prentice-Hall, 1973.
- Registry: [EQ-VV-LSQ-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-vv-lsq-001--least-squares-observed-order)

### EQ-VV-LSQ-002 — Simplified least-squares numerical uncertainty

If `0.5 <= p < p_th + 0.1` (credible order): `U = 1.25 |alpha| + U_s`;
otherwise, with `alpha_th`, `U_s,th = sqrt(S_th/(n - 2))` from the refit at
`p = p_th`: `U = 3 max(|alpha|, |alpha_th|) + max(U_s, U_s,th)`.

- Domain: as [EQ-VV-LSQ-001]; `p_th > 0` declared by the user.
- Meaning: a project simplification that keeps the structure of the
  Eca-Hoekstra procedure (fitted order, scatter term, factor of safety 1.25 or
  3) but omits their weighted fits, alternative fixed-order expansions, and
  data-range term. It is not their complete procedure.
- Code: `uq.verification.least_squares_convergence`,
  `uq.verification.verify_solution`.
- Source: [REF-ECA-HOEKSTRA-2014] (structure); simplification documented in
  this registry.
  - [REF-ECA-HOEKSTRA-2014](../references.md#ref-eca-hoekstra-2014): L. Eça and M. Hoekstra. "A Procedure for the Estimation of the Numerical Uncertainty of CFD Calculations Based on Grid Refinement Studies." *Journal of Computational Physics* 262, 104-130, 2014.

### EQ-VV-OSC-001 — Oscillatory three-grid bound

`U = F_s,osc (phi_max - phi_min)/2` with `F_s,osc = 3` by default.

- Domain: three grids with `eps32/eps21 < 0`.
- Meaning: a conservative project rule that does not use Richardson
  extrapolation when the sequence oscillates: half the oscillation range,
  multiplied by the factor of safety for untrusted estimates.
- Code: `uq.verification.verify_solution`.
- Source: [REF-ROY-2005] (oscillatory convergence invalidates Richardson
  estimates), [REF-ROACHE-1998, Chapters 4-5] (factor of safety 3); the rule
  itself is a project choice.

### EQ-VV-UNUM-001 — Numerical-uncertainty statement and standard uncertainty

Statement `phi_1 +/- U` with interval `[phi_1 - U, phi_1 + U]`;
`u_num = U / sqrt(3)` (rectangular, "uniform") or `u_num = U / 1.959964`

  V&V 20 comparison [EQ-VAL-ASME-001] and prediction [EQ-VAL-PRED-001].
