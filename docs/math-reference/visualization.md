## `probstat.visualization.accessibility`

### EQ-VIZ-WCAG-001 — Relative luminance of an sRGB colour

`L = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B`, where each channel `C` in
`{R, G, B}` is linearized from its 8-bit value `C8` by `c = C8 / 255` and
`C = c / 12.92` if `c <= 0.03928`, otherwise `C = ((c + 0.055) / 1.055)^2.4`.

- Domain: sRGB colours given as `#RRGGBB` tokens; `0 <= L <= 1`, black 0,
  white 1.
- Meaning: the luminance weight of a colour under the sRGB primaries. The
  threshold `0.03928` is the value written in WCAG 2.1; the sRGB standard
  uses `0.04045`, and no 8-bit channel value lies between the two, so both
  give the same result.
- Code: `visualization.accessibility.relative_luminance`.
- Source: [REF-W3C-WCAG21-2018, definition "relative luminance"].
  - [REF-W3C-WCAG21-2018](../references.md#ref-w3c-wcag21-2018): World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1*. W3C Recommendation, 5 June 2018. Andrew Kirkpatrick, Joshue O Connor, Alastair Campbell, and Michael Cooper (editors). [https://www.w3.org/TR/2018/REC-WCAG21-20180605/](https://www.w3.org/TR/2018/REC-WCAG21-20180605/). Definitions of relative luminance and contrast ratio; success criteria 1.1.1 (non-text content), 1.4.3 (contrast, minimum), 1.4.11 (non-text contrast), 2.4.1 (bypass blocks), and 3.1.1 (language of page).

### EQ-VIZ-WCAG-002 — Contrast ratio of two colours

`CR = (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)`, with `L1` the larger and `L2` the smaller
relative luminance [EQ-VIZ-WCAG-001] of the two colours.

- Domain: two sRGB colours; `1 <= CR <= 21`, symmetric in its arguments.
- Meaning: legibility of text or marks on a background. Level AA requires
  at least 4.5 for body text (success criterion 1.4.3), 3 for large text,
  and 3 for graphical objects such as plotted lines (criterion 1.4.11).
- Code: `visualization.accessibility.contrast_ratio`,
  `visualization.accessibility.palette_contrast`.

## `probstat.visualization.calibration_plots`

### EQ-VIZ-MIXQ-001 — Central interval of a weighted Gaussian mixture

`F(q) = sum_s w_s Phi((q - m_s) / sd_s)` with `w_s >= 0`, `sum_s w_s = 1` (a
component with `sd_s = 0` is a point mass at `m_s`); the central
`(1 - alpha)` interval is `(F^-1(alpha/2), F^-1(1 - alpha/2))`, found by
bisection because `F` is continuous and non-decreasing.

- Domain: finite means, non-negative variances and weights, `0 < alpha < 1`.
- Meaning: the exact marginal interval of a quantity that is Gaussian given a
  parameter and mixed over posterior draws (equal weights) or quadrature nodes
  (quadrature weights), such as `delta(x*)` in Kennedy-O'Hagan prediction or
  `theta_j` of the hierarchical normal model.
- Code: `visualization.calibration_plots.gaussian_mixture_interval`,
  `visualization.hierarchical_plots.theta_intervals`.
- Source: [REF-GELMAN-ETAL-2013, Section 6.3] and [REF-NIST-DLMF].
  - [REF-GELMAN-ETAL-2013](../references.md#ref-gelman-etal-2013): Andrew Gelman, John B. Carlin, Hal S. Stern, David B. Dunson, Aki Vehtari, and Donald B. Rubin. *Bayesian Data Analysis*. 3rd edition. CRC Press, 2013. ISBN 978-1-4398-4095-5.
  - [REF-NIST-DLMF](../references.md#ref-nist-dlmf): NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, Chapter 7, "Error Functions, Dawson's and Fresnel Integrals." National Institute of Standards and Technology. [https://dlmf.nist.gov/7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/7).

## `probstat.visualization.extremes_plots`

### EQ-VIZ-QQBAND-001 — Pointwise Q-Q band from uniform order statistics

`U_(i) ~ Beta(i, n - i + 1)` for the `i`-th smallest of `n` independent
uniforms; the pointwise band for the `i`-th ordered observation is
`(F^-1(b_(alpha/2)), F^-1(b_(1 - alpha/2)))` with `b_q` the `q`-quantile of that
beta law and `F` the model CDF.

- Domain: continuous model law with a quantile function; `1 <= i <= n`;
  `0 < alpha < 1`. Limits are pointwise, not simultaneous.
- Meaning: an ordered observation outside its band is unusual for its rank
  under the model; about `alpha n` points fall outside by chance.
- Code: `visualization.extremes_plots.order_statistic_band`,
  `visualization.extremes_plots.plot_tail_quantiles`.
- Source: [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002, Section 5.4].
  - [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002](../references.md#ref-casella-berger-2002): George Casella and Roger L. Berger. *Statistical Inference*. 2nd edition. Duxbury, 2002.
- Registry: [EQ-VIZ-QQBAND-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-viz-qqband-001--pointwise-q-q-band-from-uniform-order-statistics)

## `probstat.visualization.fit_plots`

### EQ-VIZ-ROOT-001 — Hanging rootogram

For count `k` with observed frequency `O_k` and fitted frequency `E_k`, the
bar spans `[sqrt(E_k) - sqrt(O_k), sqrt(E_k)]`; the curve joins the points
`(k, sqrt(E_k))`.

- Domain: non-negative observed and fitted frequencies.
- Meaning: the square root roughly stabilizes the variance of counts, and
  hanging the bars from the fitted curve moves every comparison to the
  horizontal line at zero, where departures are judged by length rather
  than against a sloping curve.
- Code: `visualization.fit_plots.hanging_rootogram_bars`,
  `visualization.fit_plots.plot_rootogram`.
- Source: [REF-KLEIBER-ZEILEIS-2016].
  - [REF-KLEIBER-ZEILEIS-2016](../references.md#ref-kleiber-zeileis-2016): Christian Kleiber and Achim Zeileis. "Visualizing Count Data Regressions Using Rootograms." *The American Statistician* 70(3), 296-303, 2016. DOI: [10.1080/00031305.2016.1173590](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2016.1173590).

## `probstat.visualization.hierarchical_plots`

### EQ-VIZ-PITBAND-001 — Pointwise binomial band of a PIT histogram

If `n` PIT values are independent and uniform on `[0, 1]`, the count in each of
`B` equal-width bins is `Binomial(n, 1/B)`; the pointwise band is
`(k_lo, k_hi)` with `k_lo` the smallest `k` such that
`P(K <= k) >= alpha/2` and `k_hi` the smallest `k` such that
`P(K <= k) >= 1 - alpha/2`, around the expected count `n/B`.

- Domain: `n >= 1`, `B >= 2`, `0 < alpha < 1`. Limits are pointwise, not
  simultaneous across bins; bin counts are dependent (they sum to `n`).
- Meaning: a bin outside its band is unusual for a calibrated predictive
  distribution; posterior (not leave-one-out) PIT values are pulled toward
  `1/2`, so a central hump is expected for in-sample checks.
- Code: `visualization.hierarchical_plots.binomial_band`,
  `visualization.hierarchical_plots.plot_pit_histogram`.
- Source: [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002, Section 3.2] and
  [REF-GNEITING-BALABDAOUI-RAFTERY-2007].
  - [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002](../references.md#ref-casella-berger-2002): George Casella and Roger L. Berger. *Statistical Inference*. 2nd edition. Duxbury, 2002.
  - [REF-GNEITING-BALABDAOUI-RAFTERY-2007](../references.md#ref-gneiting-balabdaoui-raftery-2007): Tilmann Gneiting, Fadoua Balabdaoui, and Adrian E. Raftery. ### EQ-VIZ-PITBAND-002 — Simultaneous (Bonferroni) band of a PIT histogram

With `B` equal-width bins of `n` PIT values, the simultaneous band is the
pointwise band of [EQ-VIZ-PITBAND-001] at level `1 - alpha/B` in every bin:
`k_lo` the smallest `k` with `P(K <= k) >= alpha/(2B)` and `k_hi` the smallest
`k` with `P(K <= k) >= 1 - alpha/(2B)`, `K ~ Binomial(n, 1/B)`.

- Domain: `n >= 1`, `B >= 2`, `0 < alpha < 1`.
- Meaning: each pointwise band leaves at most `alpha/B` probability outside,
  so by the union bound (Bonferroni, [EQ-INF-MT-001]) all `B` counts of
  independent uniform PIT values stay inside with probability at least
  `1 - alpha`, whatever the dependence between counts (they sum to `n`).
  The pointwise band is exceeded by about `B alpha` bins of a calibrated
  forecast; with `n = 400`, `B = 20`, `alpha = 0.05` some bin leaves it in
  about half of all calibrated samples, the simultaneous band in under 5%.
- Code: `visualization.hierarchical_plots.simultaneous_binomial_band`,
  `visualization.hierarchical_plots.plot_pit_histogram`.
- Source: [REF-LEHMANN-ROMANO-2005, Chapter 9],
  [REF-GNEITING-BALABDAOUI-RAFTERY-2007].
  - [REF-LEHMANN-ROMANO-2005](../references.md#ref-lehmann-romano-2005): E. L. Lehmann and Joseph P. Romano. *Testing Statistical Hypotheses*. 3rd edition. Springer, 2005.
  - [REF-GNEITING-BALABDAOUI-RAFTERY-2007](../references.md#ref-gneiting-balabdaoui-raftery-2007): Tilmann Gneiting, Fadoua Balabdaoui, and Adrian E. Raftery. ## `probstat.visualization.uncertainty`

### EQ-STAT-DKW-001 — Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz confidence band

`P(sup_x |F_n(x) - F(x)| > eps) <= 2 exp(-2 n eps^2)`, so
`eps = sqrt(ln(2/alpha) / (2n))` gives a simultaneous `1 - alpha` band
`[F_n - eps, F_n + eps]` clipped to `[0, 1]`.

- Domain: independent identically distributed observations from any
  continuous or discrete law; Massart's constant 2 is tight.
- Meaning: a distribution-free band for the whole CDF, wider than pointwise
  intervals because it holds for every `x` at once.
- Code: `visualization.uncertainty.dkw_epsilon`,
  `visualization.uncertainty.plot_ecdf_band`.
- Source: [REF-DVORETZKY-KIEFER-WOLFOWITZ-1956] and [REF-MASSART-1990].
  - [REF-DVORETZKY-KIEFER-WOLFOWITZ-1956](../references.md#ref-dvoretzky-kiefer-wolfowitz-1956): A. Dvoretzky, J. Kiefer, and J. Wolfowitz. "Asymptotic Minimax Character of the Sample Distribution Function and of the Classical Multinomial Estimator." *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 27(3), 642-669, 1956.
  - [REF-MASSART-1990](../references.md#ref-massart-1990): P. Massart. "The Tight Constant in the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz Inequality." *Annals of Probability* 18(3), 1269-1283, 1990.
