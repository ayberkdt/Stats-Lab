## `probstat.cost.schedule_risk`

### EQ-COST-CPM-001 — Critical path method with precedence relations and lags

`ES_j = max(0, max over predecessors i of: EF_i + l (FS), ES_i + l (SS), EF_i + l - d_j (FF))`,
`EF_j = ES_j + d_j`, `T = max_j EF_j`; backward `LF_i = min(T, min over
successors j of: LS_j - l (FS), LS_j - l + d_i (SS), LF_j - l (FF))`,
`LS_i = LF_i - d_i`; total float `TF_i = LS_i - ES_i`; free float is the
smallest slack of the outgoing relations (`ES_j - EF_i - l`, `ES_j - ES_i - l`,
`EF_j - EF_i - l`, and `T - EF_i` for a sink).

- Domain: acyclic precedence graph, finite lags, non-negative durations.
- Meaning: an activity is critical when `TF_i = 0`; a critical path is a
  chain of critical activities joined by zero-slack relations, and its
  length is the project duration `T`.
- Code: `cost.critical_path`, `cost.ActivityNetwork`, `cost.Activity`,
  `cost.Precedence`.
- Source: [REF-KELLEY-WALKER-1959].
  - [REF-KELLEY-WALKER-1959](../references.md#ref-kelley-walker-1959): James E. Kelley Jr. and Morgan R. Walker. "Critical-Path Planning and Scheduling." *Proceedings of the Eastern Joint Computer Conference*, Boston, 1959, 160-173. Confirmed bibliographically (a scan of the proceedings pages is listed by a project-history archive, not read); the forward and backward pass and the floats are re-derived, not copied.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-CPM-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-cpm-001--critical-path-method-with-precedence-relations-and-lags)

### EQ-COST-PERT-001 — Classical PERT normal approximation

`mu_T = sum_(i on path) mu_i`, `sigma_T^2 = sum_(i on path) sigma_i^2`,
`P(T <= t) ~ Phi((t - mu_T) / sigma_T)`; three-point estimates
`mu = (a + 4 m + b) / 6`, `sigma^2 = ((b - a) / 6)^2`.

- Domain: finish-to-start relations, independent durations, one critical path
  of the mean-duration network.
- Meaning: exact mean and variance for a series network of independent
  activities; otherwise optimistic, because `E max(T_1, T_2) >= max(E T_1,
  E T_2)` (Jensen) so the mean is biased low where paths merge, and the
  variance ignores the competing paths.
- Code: `cost.pert_estimate`, `cost.PertEstimate`, `cost.pert_three_point`.
- Source: [REF-MALCOLM-1959], [REF-MACCRIMMON-RYAVEC-1964].
  - [REF-MALCOLM-1959](../references.md#ref-malcolm-1959): D. G. Malcolm, J. H. Roseboom, C. E. Clark, and W. Fazar. "Application of a Technique for Research and Development Program Evaluation." *Operations Research* 7(5), 646-669, 1959.
  - [REF-MACCRIMMON-RYAVEC-1964](../references.md#ref-maccrimmon-ryavec-1964): Kenneth R. MacCrimmon and Charles A. Ryavec. "An Analytical Study of the PERT Assumptions." *Operations Research* 12(1), 16-37, 1964. DOI 10.1287/opre.12.1.16. Confirmed bibliographically (the full text is behind a paywall and was not read). Cited for the PERT assumptions and their errors; the bias of the mean at merge events is derived here from Jensen's inequality and demonstrated by simulation.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-PERT-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-pert-001--classical-pert-normal-approximation)

### EQ-COST-SRA-001 — Criticality, cruciality, and schedule sensitivity index

`CI_i = P(TF_i = 0)`, estimated by the fraction of draws with zero total
float; cruciality `rho_i = corr(d_i, T)` (Pearson, and Spearman rank);
schedule sensitivity index `SSI_i = CI_i * sd(d_i) / sd(T)`.

- Domain: `n` independent draws of the network; `rho_i` and `SSI_i` are zero
  when `d_i` or `T` is constant.
- Meaning: probability that an activity lies on a critical path (Van Slyke),
  strength of the linear (or monotone) link between its duration and the
  completion time (Williams), and the criticality-weighted share of the
  completion spread an activity can explain. The SSI is a convention of
  schedule risk software, defined here, not quoted from a source.
- Code: `cost.simulate_schedule`, `cost.ActivityRisk`.
- Source: [REF-VAN-SLYKE-1963], [REF-WILLIAMS-1992], [REF-ELMAGHRABY-2000],
  [REF-GAO-16-89G].

Confirmed bibliographically (publisher and RePEc records; the full text was not read). ### EQ-COST-SRA-002 — Completion-time distribution and deadline probability

`P(T <= t) = X / n` with `X ~ Binomial(n, P(T <= t))`; exact interval
`[Beta(alpha/2; X, n - X + 1), Beta(1 - alpha/2; X + 1, n - X)]`; quantile
interval from order statistics `[T_(l), T_(u)]` with exact coverage
`B(u - 1) - B(l - 1)`.

- Domain: independent draws; the Clopper-Pearson interval is conservative.
- Meaning: the empirical S-curve of the completion time, the confidence of
  meeting a deadline, and a distribution-free interval for any completion
  quantile.
- Code: `cost.ScheduleRiskResult.probability_by`,
  `cost.ScheduleRiskResult.quantile`, `cost.ScheduleRiskResult.s_curve`,
  `cost.DeadlineProbability`.
- Source: [REF-CLOPPER-PEARSON-1934], [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003],
  [REF-GAO-16-89G].

### EQ-COST-SRA-003 — Discrete schedule risk event

`d_i' = d_i + B * V`, `B ~ Bernoulli(p)`, `V` an impact law with quantile
function `Q`, drawn as `V = Q(U)` for `U ~ Uniform(0, 1)`.

- Domain: `0 <= p <= 1`, non-negative impacts, events independent of each
  other and of the durations.
- Meaning: a risk register entry that adds time to one activity when it
  occurs, so it enters both the critical path and the cost burn.
- Code: `cost.ScheduleRiskEvent`.
- Source: [REF-GAO-16-89G], [REF-NASA-PRA-2011].

### EQ-COST-LOAD-001 — Cost loading and joint cost-schedule confidence

`C = c_0 + sum_i F_i + sum_i r_i d_i + r_p T`, `JCL(C*, D) = P(C <= C*, T <= D)
<= min(P(C <= C*), P(T <= D))`, with equality to the product of marginals
only under independence.

- Domain: time-independent costs `F_i`, burn rates `r_i`, project rate `r_p`,
  each a number or a law drawn independently of the durations.
- Meaning: the pair (cost, completion) of one draw; because the burn terms
  grow with the durations, cost and completion are positively dependent. The
  iso-confidence frontier lists the smallest sampled cost with `JCL >= q` for
  each deadline.
- Code: `cost.cost_schedule_samples`, `cost.joint_cost_schedule_confidence`,
  `cost.cost_schedule_frontier`, `cost.IsoConfidenceFrontier`.
- Source: [REF-NASA-CEH-2015] (the joint confidence level; its appendix could
  not be opened, so the definition rests on the probability identity above).
  - [REF-NASA-CEH-2015](../references.md#ref-nasa-ceh-2015): National Aeronautics and Space Administration. *NASA Cost Estimating Handbook*, Version 4.0. NASA, Washington, DC, February 2015.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-LOAD-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-load-001--cost-loading-and-joint-cost-schedule-confidence)

## `probstat.cost.spares`

### EQ-COST-SPARE-001 — Poisson spares protection level

`D ~ Poisson(mu)`, `mu = lambda n T`; protection `P(D <= s)`; minimum
stock `min{s : P(D <= s) >= target}`.

- Domain: constant rates, no repair within the period.
- Meaning: probability that `s` spares cover the period's demand.
- Code: `cost.spare_demand_mean`, `cost.protection_level`,
  `cost.minimum_spares`.
- Source: [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004].
  - [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004](../references.md#ref-sherbrooke-2004): Craig C. Sherbrooke. *Optimal Inventory Modeling of Systems: Multi-Echelon Techniques*. 2nd edition. Kluwer Academic Publishers, 2004.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-SPARE-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-spare-001--poisson-spares-protection-level)

### EQ-COST-SPARE-002 — Expected backorders

`EBO(s) = E[(D - s)^+] = mu P(D >= s) - s P(D >= s + 1)`.

- Domain: Poisson demand (uses `k p_k = mu p_{k-1}`).
- Meaning: expected unfilled demands.
- Code: `cost.expected_backorders`.
- Source: [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004].
  - [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004](../references.md#ref-sherbrooke-2004): Craig C. Sherbrooke. *Optimal Inventory Modeling of Systems: Multi-Echelon Techniques*. 2nd edition. Kluwer Academic Publishers, 2004.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-SPARE-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-spare-002--expected-backorders)

### EQ-COST-SPARE-003 — Spare-plan cost and optimum

`TC(s) = s c_u + c_b EBO(s)`; `EBO(s) - EBO(s+1) = P(D > s)`, so the
optimum is the smallest `s` with `P(D <= s) >= 1 - c_u / c_b`.

- Domain: `c_u > 0`, `c_b >= 0`.
- Meaning: newsvendor-type stock decision.
- Code: `cost.spare_plan_cost`, `cost.optimal_spares`.
- Source: [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004].
  - [REF-SHERBROOKE-2004](../references.md#ref-sherbrooke-2004): Craig C. Sherbrooke. *Optimal Inventory Modeling of Systems: Multi-Echelon Techniques*. 2nd edition. Kluwer Academic Publishers, 2004.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-SPARE-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-spare-003--spare-plan-cost-and-optimum)

## `probstat.cost.unreliability`

### EQ-COST-UNREL-001 — Cost of unreliability

`sum_i p_i C_i` (missions); `sum_i lambda_i C_i (1 - exp(-r H)) / r`
(present value over `[0, H]`, `= sum lambda_i C_i H` at `r = 0`).

- Domain: homogeneous Poisson failures, continuous discounting.
- Meaning: expected (discounted) failure cost (Campbell's theorem).
- Code: `cost.expected_failure_cost`, `cost.cost_of_unreliability`,
  `cost.discounted_rate_factor`, `cost.compare_alternatives`,
  `cost.reliability_decision_problem`.
- Source: [REF-RAUSAND-BARROS-HOYLAND-2021], [REF-KAPLAN-GARRICK-1981].
  - [REF-RAUSAND-BARROS-HOYLAND-2021](../references.md#ref-rausand-barros-hoyland-2021): Marvin Rausand, Anne Barros, and Arnljot Høyland. *System Reliability Theory: Models, Statistical Methods, and Applications*. 3rd edition. Wiley, 2021.
  - [REF-KAPLAN-GARRICK-1981](../references.md#ref-kaplan-garrick-1981): Stanley Kaplan and B. John Garrick. "On the Quantitative Definition of Risk." *Risk Analysis* 1(1), 11-27, 1981.
- Registry: [EQ-COST-UNREL-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-cost-unrel-001--cost-of-unreliability)

### EQ-COST-CER-001 — Cost-effectiveness ratios

`CER = C / E`; `ICER = (C_b - C_a) / (E_b - E_a)`.

- Domain: `E > 0`; `E_b != E_a`.
- Meaning: cost per unit effectiveness and incremental cost per extra unit.
- Code: `cost.cost_effectiveness_ratio`, `cost.incremental_cost_effectiveness`.
- Source: [REF-FABRYCKY-BLANCHARD-1991].
