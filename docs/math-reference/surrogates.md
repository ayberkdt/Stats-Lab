## `probstat.surrogates.bayesian_quadrature`

### EQ-SUR-BQ-001 — Bayesian quadrature posterior

For `f ~ GP(m, k)`, `y_i = f(x_i)`, and a probability measure `pi`:
`z_i = int k(x, x_i) pi(dx)`, `kk = int int k(x, x') pi(dx) pi(dx')`,
`E[I | y] = m + z' K^-1 (y - m 1)`, `Var[I | y] = kk - z' K^-1 z`.

- Domain: positive-definite `K` (plus an optional noise diagonal); kernel
  hyperparameters fixed.
- Meaning: `I = int f dpi` is a linear functional of a Gaussian process, so
  it is Gaussian; the weights `K^-1 z` do not depend on the data, and the
  variance cannot increase when nodes are added.
- Code: `surrogates.bayesian_quadrature`,
  `surrogates.BayesianQuadratureResult.standard_deviation`.
- Source: [REF-OHAGAN-1991].
  - [REF-OHAGAN-1991](../references.md#ref-ohagan-1991): Anthony O'Hagan. "Bayes-Hermite Quadrature." *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* 29, 245-260, 1991. Posterior mean and variance of an integral against a Gaussian measure under a Gaussian-process prior on the integrand.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-BQ-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-bq-001--bayesian-quadrature-posterior)

### EQ-SUR-BQ-002 — Squared-exponential kernel integrals, Gaussian measure

For `k(x, x') = exp(-(x - x')^2 / (2 ell^2))` and `pi = N(b, s^2)`:
`z(x) = sqrt(ell^2 / (ell^2 + s^2)) exp(-(x - b)^2 / (2 (ell^2 + s^2)))`,
`kk = sqrt(ell^2 / (ell^2 + 2 s^2))`. In `d` dimensions with a product
measure both are products over the coordinates, times `sigma_f^2`.

- Domain: `ell > 0`, `s > 0`.
- Meaning: the kernel is `ell sqrt(2 pi)` times the `N(x', ell^2)` density,
  so `z` is a Gaussian convolution; `kk` follows from `X - X' ~ N(0, 2 s^2)`.
- Code: `surrogates.kernel_mean`, `surrogates.kernel_double_integral`.
- Source: [REF-OHAGAN-1991] for the rule; the closed forms are standard
  Gaussian integrals, verified against `numerics.integrate` in
  `tests/test_multifidelity_surrogates.py`.
  - [REF-OHAGAN-1991](../references.md#ref-ohagan-1991): Anthony O'Hagan. "Bayes-Hermite Quadrature." *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* 29, 245-260, 1991. Posterior mean and variance of an integral against a Gaussian measure under a Gaussian-process prior on the integrand.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-BQ-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-bq-002--squared-exponential-kernel-integrals-gaussian-measure)

### EQ-SUR-BQ-003 — Squared-exponential kernel integrals, uniform measure

For the same kernel and `pi` uniform on `[a, c]`, `w = c - a`:
`z(x) = ell sqrt(pi/2) / w (erf((c - x) / (sqrt(2) ell))
- erf((a - x) / (sqrt(2) ell)))`,
`kk = (2 / w^2) (w ell sqrt(pi/2) erf(w / (sqrt(2) ell))
- ell^2 (1 - exp(-w^2 / (2 ell^2))))`.

- Domain: `ell > 0`, `a < c`.
- Meaning: `z` integrates a Gaussian bump over an interval; `kk` uses the
  triangular density `2 (w - v) / w^2` of `|X - X'|` on `[0, w]`.
- Code: `surrogates.kernel_mean`, `surrogates.kernel_double_integral`.
- Source: derived for this library from [REF-NIST-DLMF, Section 7.2] (error
  function); verified against `numerics.integrate` in
  `tests/test_multifidelity_surrogates.py`.
  - [REF-NIST-DLMF](../references.md#ref-nist-dlmf): NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, Chapter 7, "Error Functions, Dawson's and Fresnel Integrals." National Institute of Standards and Technology. [https://dlmf.nist.gov/7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/7).
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-BQ-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-bq-003--squared-exponential-kernel-integrals-uniform-measure)

## `probstat.surrogates.ensemble`

### EQ-SUR-DE-001 — Gaussian negative log-likelihood of a mean-variance network

`l(theta; x, y) = (log(2 pi sigma^2(x)) + (y - mu(x))^2 / sigma^2(x)) / 2`
with `(mu(x), s(x))` the two outputs of an MLP [EQ-NN-MLP-001] and
`sigma^2(x) = exp(clip(s(x), -20, 20)) + 1e-6` (standardized units); a
homoscedastic member replaces `s(x)` by one learned scalar. Each member
minimizes `L(theta) = mean_i l(theta; x_i, y_i) + (lambda / 2) sum w^2` over
the weights `w` by Adam [EQ-OPT-ADAM-002] on mini-batches.

- Domain: standardized inputs and outputs; `lambda >= 0`.
- Meaning: the negative log-likelihood is a proper scoring rule, so it is
  minimized by the true conditional mean and variance; the variance output
  absorbs the noise the network cannot explain. The clip and the floor keep
  `sigma^2` and its inverse finite (computational choices). The source
  passes the variance output through a softplus with a `1e-6` floor; the
  log-variance form is a parameterization choice with the same optimum.
- Code: `surrogates.fit_deep_ensemble`,
  `surrogates.DeepEnsemble.member_predictions`.
- Source: [REF-LAKSHMINARAYANAN-PRITZEL-BLUNDELL-2017, Section 2.2.1 and
  Algorithm 1]; [REF-GOODFELLOW-BENGIO-COURVILLE-2016, Section 7.1.1] for the
  weight penalty.
  - [REF-LAKSHMINARAYANAN-PRITZEL-BLUNDELL-2017](../references.md#ref-lakshminarayanan-pritzel-blundell-2017): Balaji Lakshminarayanan, Alexander Pritzel, and Charles Blundell. "Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles." In *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 30* (NIPS 2017), 6402-6413, 2017. Networks with a Gaussian mean-variance output trained on the negative log-likelihood (a proper scoring rule) from independent random initializations, combined as a uniformly weighted Gaussian mixture. Preprint: [arXiv:1612.01474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474).
  - [REF-GOODFELLOW-BENGIO-COURVILLE-2016](../references.md#ref-goodfellow-bengio-courville-2016): Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville. *Deep Learning*. MIT Press, 2016. ISBN 978-0-262-03561-3. [https://www.deeplearningbook.org](https://www.deeplearningbook.org).
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-DE-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-de-001--gaussian-negative-log-likelihood-of-a-mean-variance-network)

### EQ-SUR-DE-002 — Deep-ensemble mixture prediction and variance split

`mu*(x) = (1/M) sum_m mu_m(x)`,
`sigma*^2(x) = (1/M) sum_m (sigma_m^2(x) + mu_m^2(x)) - mu*^2(x)
= (1/M) sum_m sigma_m^2(x) + (1/M) sum_m (mu_m(x) - mu*(x))^2`.

- Domain: `M >= 1` members fitted by [EQ-SUR-DE-001]; all quantities in
  original units after undoing the output standardization.
- Meaning: the mean and variance of the uniform mixture of the member
  Gaussians. The first term (aleatoric) is the noise the members attribute
  to the data; the second (epistemic) is the disagreement between members,
  which is small where the data constrain the networks and grows away from
  them. Neither term is a guaranteed bound on the actual error.
- Code: `surrogates.DeepEnsemble.predict_decomposed`,
  `surrogates.DeepEnsemble.predict_with_variance`,
  `surrogates.DeepEnsemble.epistemic_variance`,
  `surrogates.DeepEnsemble.aleatoric_variance`.
- Source: [REF-LAKSHMINARAYANAN-PRITZEL-BLUNDELL-2017, Section 2.4].

"Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles." In *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 30* (NIPS 2017), 6402-6413, 2017. ### EQ-SUR-GP-004 — GP log marginal likelihood and gradient

`log p(y|theta) = -(1/2) y'K^-1 y - (1/2) log det K - (n/2) log 2pi`;
`d/d theta_j = (1/2) tr((alpha alpha' - K^-1) dK/d theta_j)` with
`theta = (log sigma_f^2, log ell_1..d[, log sigma_n^2])`;
`dk/d log ell_i = sigma_f^2 (Delta_i/ell_i)^2 exp(-r^2/2)` (squared
exponential) or `(5/3) sigma_f^2 (Delta_i/ell_i)^2 (1 + sqrt(5) r) exp(-sqrt(5) r)`
(Matern 5/2).

- Domain: positive-definite `K`; log parameters restricted to `|theta_j| <= 25`.
- Meaning: type-II maximum likelihood objective balancing data fit and
  complexity; maximized by BFGS with seeded restarts.
- Code: `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_marginal_likelihood`,
  `surrogates.fit_gaussian_process`.
- Source: [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006, Section 5.4].

### EQ-SUR-GP-006 — Regularized GP hyperparameter estimation

With per-input training extent `E_i` and smallest positive coordinate gap
`h_i`, maximize `log p(y | theta) + log pi(theta)` over the box
`log ell_i in [log(a h_i), log(b E_i)]`, `log sigma_f^2 in [log s_lo, log s_hi]`
(`log sigma_n^2 in [-25, log s_hi]` when estimated), all clipped to
`|theta_j| <= 25`, with kernel matrix
`K = sigma_f^2 (R + tau I) + (nugget + sigma_n^2) I` and optional log-normal
prior `log pi = -(1/2) sum_i ((log ell_i - log E_i) / s)^2`. The box is
searched through `theta_j = l_j + (u_j - l_j) sin^2(phi_j / 2)`, so
`d theta_j / d phi_j = (1/2)(u_j - l_j) sin(phi_j)`; a parameter within
`0.01` of a bound is reported. The relative nugget adds
`(1/2) tau sigma_f^2 tr(alpha alpha' - K^-1)` to `d log p / d log sigma_f^2`.

- Domain: `a, b > 0`, `0 < s_lo < s_hi`, `tau >= 0`, `s > 0`; defaults
  `a = 0.1`, `b = 20`, `[s_lo, s_hi] = [1e-3, 1e3]` (standardized output
  units), `tau = 1e-8`, no prior. These values are computational choices.
- Meaning: below the smallest gap all correlations vanish and the likelihood
  is flat in `ell_i` (white-noise optimum); far above the extent `K` tends to
  a singular polynomial limit whose likelihood keeps rising while the
  posterior variance falls below rounding. `tau` bounds the condition number
  of `R + tau I` by `1 + n / tau`, i.e. caps the signal-to-noise ratio at
  `1 / tau`.
- Code: `surrogates.GaussianProcessRegularization`,
  `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_parameter_bounds`,
  `surrogates.fit_gaussian_process`,
  `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_marginal_likelihood`,
  `reliability.ak_mcs`.
- Source: [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006, Sections 5.2 and 5.4],
  [REF-ANDRIANAKIS-CHALLENOR-2012].
  - [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006](../references.md#ref-rasmussen-williams-2006): Carl Edward Rasmussen and Christopher K. I. Williams. *Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning*. MIT Press, 2006.
  - [REF-ANDRIANAKIS-CHALLENOR-2012](../references.md#ref-andrianakis-challenor-2012): Ioannis Andrianakis and Peter G. Challenor. "The Effect of the Nugget on Gaussian Process Emulators of Computer Models." *Computational Statistics & Data Analysis* 56(12), 4215-4228, 2012. DOI: [10.1016/j.csda.2012.04.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2012.04.020).
- Also names: [`reliability`](reliability.md)
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-GP-006](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-gp-006--regularized-gp-hyperparameter-estimation)

### EQ-SUR-GPX-001 — Kernel algebra and exact kernel gradients

`k = k_1 + k_2`, `k = k_1 k_2`; `dk/dtheta = (dk_1/dtheta_1, dk_2/dtheta_2)` for a sum and `(k_2 dk_1/dtheta_1, k_1 dk_2/dtheta_2)` for a product, with `theta` the log hyperparameters.

- Domain: `k_1`, `k_2` positive semidefinite kernels with positive hyperparameters stored as logs.
- Meaning: sums and (Schur) products of kernels are kernels; the gradient with respect to the log parameters is exact and feeds the marginal-likelihood gradient. A white component cannot be a product factor.

### EQ-SUR-GPX-002 — Periodic, rational-quadratic, linear and white kernels

`k_per = s^2 exp(-2 sin^2(pi r/p)/l^2)`, `k_RQ = s^2 (1 + r^2/(2 a l^2))^(-a)`, `k_lin = b + v x.y`, `k_white = s_n^2 [i = j]`, with `r = ||x - y||`.

- Domain: all hyperparameters positive; the white variance is added to the diagonal of training matrices only.
- Meaning: periodic structure of period `p`, a scale mixture of squared exponentials (tends to the SE kernel as `a -> infinity`), a random affine function, and observation noise. ### EQ-SUR-GPX-004 — Derivative kernel blocks

For `k = s^2 phi(q)`, `q = sum_i (D_i/l_i)^2`, `D = x - y`: `cov(d_i f(x), f(y)) = s^2 phi'(q) 2 D_i/l_i^2`, `cov(f(x), d_j f(y)) = -s^2 phi'(q) 2 D_j/l_j^2`, `cov(d_i f(x), d_j f(y)) = s^2 [-2 phi'(q) delta_ij/l_i^2 - 4 phi''(q) D_i D_j/(l_i^2 l_j^2)]`.

- Domain: squared exponential, Matern 5/2, Matern 3/2 (for the last, `phi''` diverges like `1/r` at `r = 0` but `phi'' D_i D_j -> 0`).
- Meaning: covariances between a smooth GP and its gradient; in 1-D for the SE kernel `d^2k/dx dy = k (1/l^2 - D^2/l^4)`. Verified against finite differences.
- Code: `surrogates.gp_advanced.derivative_kernel_blocks`.

### EQ-SUR-GPANOM-001 — One-step-ahead predictive law and standardized residual

### EQ-SUR-GPANOM-004 — Split-conformal alarm threshold

`q = S_(r)` with `r = ceil((1 - alpha)(n + 1))`, the `r`-th smallest of `n` calibration scores; alarm when `S_new > q`. For exchangeable scores `P(S_new > q) <= alpha`, and `>= alpha - 1/(n + 1)` for continuous scores.

- Domain: calibration scores from held-out nominal data (disjoint from the training window), `r <= n` (otherwise `ValueError`; the library never returns an infinite threshold silently). The rank is computed with exact rational arithmetic on the decimal value of `alpha`.

Telemetry is a time series: one-step-ahead residuals of a correct model are approximately independent but not exchangeable in general, so the guarantee is approximate and the realized false-alarm rate must be measured on fresh nominal data (`false_alarm_rate`), as the tests do. ### EQ-SUR-GPANOM-006 — Student-t observation noise by EM (outlier-robust training)

- Meaning: samples far from the fit receive a small weight `w_i` and therefore a large noise variance, so isolated outliers in the nominal training window do not inflate the noise estimate or bend the fitted function. ### EQ-SUR-GPANOM-007 — Sliding-window Cholesky update in `O(w^2)`

### EQ-SUR-LFM-001 — First-order latent force model and its mean

`dy_d/dt + D_d y_d = B_d + S_d f(t)`, `y_d(t) = y_d(0) e^(-D_d t) + B_d/D_d (1 - e^(-D_d t)) + S_d int_0^t e^(-D_d (t - tau)) f(tau) dtau`; with `f ~ GP(0, k)`, `k(t, t') = exp(-(t - t')^2 / (2 l^2))`, the mean is `m_d(t) = B_d/D_d + (y_d(0) - B_d/D_d) e^(-D_d t)`.

- Domain: `D_d > 0` (1/time), `S_d`, `B_d` real, `l > 0`, `t >= 0`; the ODE starts at `t = 0` and the default initial state is equilibrium `y_d(0) = B_d / D_d`, which makes the mean constant. The force has unit prior variance, so `S_d f` is the input felt by channel `d`.
- Meaning: the outputs are a linear functional of one Gaussian process, hence jointly Gaussian with the covariances of [EQ-SUR-LFM-002] and [EQ-SUR-LFM-003]. Only the products `S_d f` and a global sign are identified.
- Code: `surrogates.latent_force.lfm_mean`.

### EQ-SUR-LFM-002 — Output-force covariance (erf form)

`Cov(y(t), f(t')) = S l sqrt(pi/2) exp(D^2 l^2 / 2 - D (t - t')) [erf((t - t' - D l^2) / (sqrt(2) l)) + erf((t' + D l^2) / (sqrt(2) l))]`.

- Domain: `D > 0`, `l > 0`, `t, t' >= 0`; implemented for `D l <= 10` (larger products overflow `exp(D^2 l^2 / 2)`); the sum of erf values is evaluated as `erfc(-a) - erfc(b)` and the exponential is combined with the large-argument `erfc` asymptotics, which avoids cancellation and overflow.
- Meaning: complete the square in `-D (t - tau) - (tau - t')^2 / (2 l^2)` and integrate the Gaussian over `tau in [0, t]`. It vanishes at `t = 0`, is the cross term used for the force posterior in [EQ-SUR-LFM-005], and tends to `(S / D) k(t - t')` for `D l` large.
- Code: `surrogates.latent_force.lfm_force_covariance`.

### EQ-SUR-LFM-003 — Output-output covariance (four erf pairs)

With `s = sqrt(2) l`, `P(E, a, b) = e^E (erf(a) + erf(b))`: `Cov(y_d(t), y_d'(t')) = S S' l sqrt(pi/2) / (D + D') [ P(D'^2 l^2/2 + D'(t - t'), (t' - t - D' l^2)/s, (t + D' l^2)/s) + P(D^2 l^2/2 + D(t' - t), (t - t' - D l^2)/s, (t' + D l^2)/s) - P(D'^2 l^2/2 - D t - D' t', (t' - D' l^2)/s, D' l/sqrt(2)) - P(D^2 l^2/2 - D t - D' t', (t - D l^2)/s, D l/sqrt(2)) ]`.

- Domain: as [EQ-SUR-LFM-002] for both decays.
- Meaning: `K = S int_0^t e^(-D (t - tau)) c'(t', tau) dtau` with the output-force covariance `c'` of [EQ-SUR-LFM-002]; the integrals `int e^(a x) erf(b x + c) dx` are elementary, so the double convolution has a closed form. It is symmetric under `(t, D, S) <-> (t', D', S')`, vanishes at `t = 0` or `t' = 0`, and tends to `S S' k(t - t') / (D D')` for large decays. Verified in the tests against Gauss-Legendre double quadrature and Monte Carlo of the ODE.

### EQ-SUR-LFM-004 — Latent force marginal likelihood and exact gradient

`log p(y | theta) = -(1/2) r' (K + N)^-1 r - (1/2) log|K + N| - (n/2) log(2 pi)`, `r = y - m`, `N = diag(sigma_c^2)`; `d log p / d theta_i = (1/2) alpha' (dK_i) alpha - (1/2) tr((K + N)^-1 dK_i) + (dm_i)' alpha`, `alpha = (K + N)^-1 r`.

- Domain: `theta = (log D_c, S_c, B_c, log l, log sigma_c^2)`; `dK` follows from the analytic derivatives of [EQ-SUR-LFM-003] with respect to `D`, `D'`, `l` (each erf pair `e^E (erfc(-a) - erfc(b))` has the derivative `value dE + (2/sqrt(pi)) (e^(E - a^2) da + e^(E - b^2) db)`) and trivially with respect to `S_c` and the noise; the fit works with `mu_c = B_c / D_c` (chain rule `d/dlog D |_mu = d/dlog D |_B + B d/dB`, `d/dmu = D d/dB`).
- Meaning: type-II maximum likelihood of the hybrid model; maximized with BFGS. The sensitivities and the force are identified only through `S_c f` and a global sign, and absolute decay rates are partly confounded with the smoothness of the unknown force (several channels sharing the force identify ratios).

### EQ-SUR-LFM-005 — Posterior of the outputs and of the latent force

### EQ-SUR-LFM-006 — Matern force as a linear SDE

`dz = F z dt + L dW`, `f = z_0`, `L = e_last`, spectral density `q`, stationary covariance `P_inf` with `F P_inf + P_inf F' + q L L' = 0` and `[P_inf exp(F tau)']_(0,0) = k_nu(|tau|)`; with `lam = sqrt(2 nu) / l`: `nu = 1/2`: `F = [-lam]`, `q = 2 lam`, `P_inf = [1]`; `nu = 3/2`: `F = [[0, 1], [-lam^2, -2 lam]]`, `q = 4 lam^3`, `P_inf = diag(1, lam^2)`; `nu = 5/2`: `F = [[0, 1, 0], [0, 0, 1], [-lam^3, -3 lam^2, -3 lam]]`, `q = 16 lam^5 / 3`, `P_inf = [[1, 0, -lam^2/3], [0, lam^2/3, 0], [-lam^2/3, 0, lam^4]]`.

- Domain: `nu in {1/2, 3/2, 5/2}`, unit variance, `l > 0`.
- Meaning: the Matern process is the first component of a linear time-invariant SDE, so coupling it to a linear ODE gives a linear-Gaussian state-space model. The Lyapunov residual and the kernel `[P_inf exp(F tau)']_(0,0)` are checked in the tests.

### EQ-SUR-LFM-007 — Kalman evidence and smoother of the state-space latent force model

Joint state `x = (y_1, y_1', ..., z)`, `dx = F x dt + L dW`; discretization `A = exp(F dt)`, `Q = int_0^dt exp(F s) (q L L') exp(F' s) ds` (Van Loan block exponential of `[[F, q L L'], [0, -F']] dt`); scalar updates `s = P_ii + sigma^2`, `e = y - m_i`, `K = P e_i / s`; `log p(y) = sum -(log(2 pi s) + e^2 / s) / 2`; smoother `r_(j-1) = z' e_j / s_j + L_j' r_j`, `N_(j-1) = z' z / s_j + L_j' N_j L_j`, `L_j = T_j (I - K_j z)`, `E x_j = a_j + P_j r_(j-1)`, `Var x_j = P_j - P_j N_(j-1) P_j`.

- Domain: `P_0` has zero variance in the output states (equilibrium start) and the stationary `P_inf` in the force states; observations may be heterotopic; diagonal noise.
- Meaning: the Kalman filter returns the exact GP evidence and the Bryson-Frazier (adjoint) smoother the exact posterior marginals of the outputs and the force, both in `O(n)`; no predicted covariance is inverted. The tests compare both with a dense GP built from the Green's function convolution with the Matern kernel (evidence to 1e-8 and marginals to 2e-6).

### EQ-SUR-MF-001 — Recursive AR(1) co-kriging predictor

`Z_1 ~ GP(b_1, k_1)` and, for `t = 2..s`,
`Z_t(x) = rho_(t-1) Z_(t-1)(x) + delta_t(x)` with
`delta_t ~ GP(b_t, k_t)` independent of `Z_(t-1)`. On nested designs
`D_s subset ... subset D_1`, with `(m_1, s_1^2)` the GP posterior of `Z_1`
given `z_1` and `(m_delta_t, s_delta_t^2)` the GP posterior of `delta_t`
given `z_t - rho_(t-1) z_(t-1)(D_t)`:
`m_t(x) = rho_(t-1) m_(t-1)(x) + m_delta_t(x)`,
`s_t^2(x) = rho_(t-1)^2 s_(t-1)^2(x) + s_delta_t^2(x)`.

- Domain: nested designs (every point of `D_t` is a point of `D_(t-1)`);
  `rho`, `b_t`, and the kernel hyperparameters fixed (plug-in variance).
- Meaning: the map `(z_1, ..., z_s) -> (z_1, z_2 - rho_1 z_1(D_2), ...)` is
  a bijection onto independent data sets, so the joint Gaussian conditional
  with the block AR(1) covariance factorizes into `s` ordinary GP
  regressions; the recursion equals the joint co-kriging predictor.
- Code: `surrogates.RecursiveCoKriging.level_predictions`,
  `surrogates.RecursiveCoKriging.predict_with_variance`,
  `surrogates.RecursiveCoKriging.predict`,
  `surrogates.fit_recursive_cokriging`,
  `surrogates.gaussian_process_from_hyperparameters`.

### EQ-SUR-NARGP-002 — Structured NARGP kernel

`k_t((x, f), (x', f')) = k_rho(x, x') k_f(f, f') + k_delta(x, x')`, with
`k_rho` a unit-variance stationary ARD kernel in `x`, `k_f` a stationary
kernel in the scalar `f` with variance `s_f^2`, and `k_delta` a stationary ARD
kernel in `x` with variance `s_delta^2`; `2 d + 3` log hyperparameters
`(ell_rho, s_f^2, ell_f, s_delta^2, ell_delta)`.

- Domain: positive hyperparameters; positive semi-definite as a sum of a

### EQ-SUR-UK-001 — Universal kriging trend and process variance

  likelihood process variance for given length scales; a constant regressor
  gives ordinary kriging.

### EQ-SUR-UK-002 — Concentrated kriging likelihood and gradient

`psi(theta) = (1/2)(N log sigma^2(theta) + log det R(theta))`, minimized over

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-001 — Least-angle regression step

On centred unit-norm columns `X` with correlations `c = X'(y - mu)`,
`C = max_j |c_j|`, active set `A` and signs `s_j = sign(c_j)`:
`G_A = X_A' X_A`, `A_A = (s' G_A^-1 s)^(-1/2)`,
`u_A = A_A X_A G_A^-1 s`, `a = X' u_A`, and `mu <- mu + gamma u_A` with
`gamma = min+_(j not in A) {(C - c_j)/(A_A - a_j), (C + c_j)/(A_A + a_j)}`,
or `gamma = C / A_A` (least squares on `A`) when no column may enter.

- Domain: `N >= 2` rows; columns constant on the rows, or numerically in
  the span of the active ones, never enter; at most `min(m, N - 1)` steps.
- Meaning: every active column keeps the same absolute correlation with
  the residual, which falls until an inactive column ties; the entry order
  ranks the regressors. Centring absorbs the intercept (the constant PCE
  term, which is always kept and not ranked).
- Code: `surrogates.least_angle_regression`,
  `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples`.
- Source: [REF-EFRON-HASTIE-JOHNSTONE-TIBSHIRANI-2004].

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-002 — Orthogonal matching pursuit selection

`j_(k+1) = argmax_(j not in A_k) |psi_j' r_k| / ||psi_j - mean(psi_j)||`,
`r_k = y - Psi_(A_k) c_(A_k)` the least-squares residual on the constant
and the `k` terms selected so far.

- Domain: as [EQ-SUR-SPCE-001]; the residual is recomputed by least squares
  after every addition.
- Meaning: greedy forward selection of the term most correlated with what
  the current fit misses; the nested sets are then scored by
  [EQ-SUR-SPCE-003] exactly as for LAR. It fits the training runs more
  aggressively than LAR, so its corrected LOO error is more optimistic.
- Code: `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples` (`method="omp"`).
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011] for the LOO-based choice of the nested
  set; the selection rule is the standard orthogonal matching pursuit.
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-002--orthogonal-matching-pursuit-selection)

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-004 — Hybrid LAR selection over degrees and q-norms

`(p*, q*, k*) = argmin epsilon*_LOO(A_k(p, q))` over the requested degrees
`p`, norms `q`, and steps `k`, where `A_k(p, q)` is the constant plus the
first `k` terms ranked by [EQ-SUR-SPCE-001] (or [EQ-SUR-SPCE-002]) on the
truncation set `A_(p,q)` of [EQ-SUR-PCE-004], each refitted by
[EQ-SUR-PCE-005].

- Domain: `N >= 3`; `k <= N - 2`, so the set may be larger than the design.
- Meaning: LAR only ranks the terms; ordinary least squares on each nested
  set removes the LAR shrinkage, and the corrected LOO error chooses the
  sparsity, the degree, and the norm. Early stopping (a computational
  choice) cuts a path after `max(10, ceil(0.1 k_max))` steps without
  improvement and the degree loop after two degrees without improvement.
- Code: `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples`,
  `surrogates.fit_sparse_polynomial_chaos`,
  `surrogates.SparseChaosSelection`.
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011].
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-004](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-004--hybrid-lar-selection-over-degrees-and-q-norms)

## `probstat.surrogates.stochastic_kriging`

### EQ-SUR-SK-001 — Stochastic kriging predictor

`Ybar_i = (1/n_i) sum_j Y_j(x_i)`, `Sigma = S_M + S_e` with
`S_M[i][j] = tau^2 r(x_i, x_j)` and `S_e = diag(V(x_i) / n_i)`, `V(x_i)`
replaced by the sample variance of the replications:
`Yhat(x0) = b0 + S_M(x0, .)' Sigma^-1 (Ybar - b0 1)`; when `b0` is not given,
`b0 = 1' Sigma^-1 Ybar / 1' Sigma^-1 1`.

- Domain: independent replications (no common random numbers), `n_i >= 2`
  to estimate `V(x_i)`; `Sigma` positive definite.
- Meaning: best linear predictor of the mean response from sample means with
  heteroscedastic noise; with equal `V / n` it is GP regression with a
  homoscedastic noise variance.
- Code: `surrogates.StochasticKriging.predict_with_variance`,
  `surrogates.StochasticKriging.predict`,
  `surrogates.fit_stochastic_kriging`,
  `surrogates.stochastic_kriging_from_summaries`.
- Source: [REF-ANKENMAN-NELSON-STAUM-2010].
  - [REF-ANKENMAN-NELSON-STAUM-2010](../references.md#ref-ankenman-nelson-staum-2010): Bruce Ankenman, Barry L. Nelson, and Jeremy Staum. "Stochastic Kriging for Simulation Metamodeling." *Operations Research* 58(2), 371-382, 2010. Kriging of the sample means of replicated stochastic simulation runs with an extrinsic spatial covariance and an intrinsic diagonal covariance of the simulation noise estimated by the sample variances. DOI: [10.1287/opre.1090.0754](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1090.0754).
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SK-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-sk-001--stochastic-kriging-predictor)

### EQ-SUR-SK-002 — Stochastic kriging mean squared error

With `c = S_M(x0, .)`:
`MSE(x0) = tau^2 - c' Sigma^-1 c + (1 - 1' Sigma^-1 c)^2 / (1' Sigma^-1 1)`;
the last term is dropped when `b0` is known.

- Domain: `S_e` and the kernel hyperparameters treated as known.
- Meaning: variance of `Y(x0) - Yhat(x0)` for the mean response surface; the
  last term is the variance contribution of the generalized least-squares
  estimate of `b0`, obtained from the unbiased linear predictor with weights
  `lambda = Sigma^-1 c + Sigma^-1 1 (1 - 1' Sigma^-1 c) / (1' Sigma^-1 1)`.
- Code: `surrogates.StochasticKriging.predict_with_variance`.
- Source: [REF-ANKENMAN-NELSON-STAUM-2010].

DOI: [10.1287/opre.1090.0754](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1090.0754).

### EQ-SUR-SK-003 — Profile likelihood of the extrinsic hyperparameters

`log L(tau^2, ell) = -(1/2) (log det Sigma + (Ybar - b0 1)' Sigma^-1
(Ybar - b0 1) + k log 2 pi)` with `Sigma = S_M(tau^2, ell) + S_e`, `S_e`
fixed, and `b0` the generalized least-squares estimate at each
`(tau^2, ell)`.

- Domain: at least two design points with distinct sample means and
  non-constant inputs.
- Meaning: Gaussian likelihood of the sample means with the intrinsic
  variances plugged in.
