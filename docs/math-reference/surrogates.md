### EQ-SUR-GP-004 — GP log marginal likelihood and gradient

`log p(y|theta) = -(1/2) y'K^-1 y - (1/2) log det K - (n/2) log 2pi`;
`d/d theta_j = (1/2) tr((alpha alpha' - K^-1) dK/d theta_j)` with
`theta = (log sigma_f^2, log ell_1..d[, log sigma_n^2])`;
`dk/d log ell_i = sigma_f^2 (Delta_i/ell_i)^2 exp(-r^2/2)` (squared
exponential) or `(5/3) sigma_f^2 (Delta_i/ell_i)^2 (1 + sqrt(5) r) exp(-sqrt(5) r)`
(Matern 5/2).

- Domain: positive-definite `K`; log parameters restricted to `|theta_j| <= 25`.
- Meaning: type-II maximum likelihood objective balancing data fit and
  complexity; maximized by BFGS with seeded restarts.
- Code: `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_marginal_likelihood`,
  `surrogates.fit_gaussian_process`.
- Source: [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006, Section 5.4].

### EQ-SUR-GP-006 — Regularized GP hyperparameter estimation

With per-input training extent `E_i` and smallest positive coordinate gap
`h_i`, maximize `log p(y | theta) + log pi(theta)` over the box
`log ell_i in [log(a h_i), log(b E_i)]`, `log sigma_f^2 in [log s_lo, log s_hi]`
(`log sigma_n^2 in [-25, log s_hi]` when estimated), all clipped to
`|theta_j| <= 25`, with kernel matrix
`K = sigma_f^2 (R + tau I) + (nugget + sigma_n^2) I` and optional log-normal
prior `log pi = -(1/2) sum_i ((log ell_i - log E_i) / s)^2`. The box is
searched through `theta_j = l_j + (u_j - l_j) sin^2(phi_j / 2)`, so
`d theta_j / d phi_j = (1/2)(u_j - l_j) sin(phi_j)`; a parameter within
`0.01` of a bound is reported. The relative nugget adds
`(1/2) tau sigma_f^2 tr(alpha alpha' - K^-1)` to `d log p / d log sigma_f^2`.

- Domain: `a, b > 0`, `0 < s_lo < s_hi`, `tau >= 0`, `s > 0`; defaults
  `a = 0.1`, `b = 20`, `[s_lo, s_hi] = [1e-3, 1e3]` (standardized output
  units), `tau = 1e-8`, no prior. These values are computational choices.
- Meaning: below the smallest gap all correlations vanish and the likelihood
  is flat in `ell_i` (white-noise optimum); far above the extent `K` tends to
  a singular polynomial limit whose likelihood keeps rising while the
  posterior variance falls below rounding. `tau` bounds the condition number
  of `R + tau I` by `1 + n / tau`, i.e. caps the signal-to-noise ratio at
  `1 / tau`.
- Code: `surrogates.GaussianProcessRegularization`,
  `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_parameter_bounds`,
  `surrogates.fit_gaussian_process`,
  `surrogates.gaussian_process_log_marginal_likelihood`,
  `reliability.ak_mcs`.
- Source: [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006, Sections 5.2 and 5.4],
  [REF-ANDRIANAKIS-CHALLENOR-2012].
  - [REF-RASMUSSEN-WILLIAMS-2006](../references.md#ref-rasmussen-williams-2006): Carl Edward Rasmussen and Christopher K. I. Williams. *Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning*. MIT Press, 2006.
  - [REF-ANDRIANAKIS-CHALLENOR-2012](../references.md#ref-andrianakis-challenor-2012): Ioannis Andrianakis and Peter G. Challenor. "The Effect of the Nugget on Gaussian Process Emulators of Computer Models." *Computational Statistics & Data Analysis* 56(12), 4215-4228, 2012. DOI: [10.1016/j.csda.2012.04.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2012.04.020).
- Also names: [`reliability`](reliability.md)

## `probstat.surrogates.pc_kriging`

### EQ-SUR-UK-001 — Universal kriging trend and process variance

`Y(x) = f(x)' beta + sigma^2 Z(x)` with `Z` a unit-variance stationary
process of correlation `R(x, x'; ell)`; for `N` runs, regression matrix
`F` (`N x P`) and correlation matrix `R` (diagonal `1 + nugget`):
`beta = (F'R^-1F)^-1 F'R^-1 Y`,
`sigma^2 = (1/N) (Y - F beta)' R^-1 (Y - F beta)`; computed with
`R = L L'`, `F~ = L^-1 F`, `Y~ = L^-1 Y` as the least-squares problem
`min ||Y~ - F~ beta||`.

- Domain: `N > P`; `R` positive definite; `F~` of full column rank (a
  whitened column whose squared sine to the earlier ones is below `1e-12`
  is refused).
- Meaning: generalized least squares for the trend and the maximum
  likelihood process variance for given length scales; a constant regressor
  gives ordinary kriging.
- Code: `surrogates.UniversalKriging`, `surrogates.fit_universal_kriging`.

### EQ-SUR-UK-002 — Concentrated kriging likelihood and gradient

`psi(theta) = (1/2)(N log sigma^2(theta) + log det R(theta))`, minimized over
`theta = (log ell_1..d[, log nugget])`, which is minimizing
`sigma^2 det(R)^(1/N)`; `d psi / d theta_j = (1/2) tr(W dR/d theta_j)` with
`W = R^-1 - alpha alpha' / sigma^2`, `alpha = R^-1 (Y - F beta)`; the trend
coefficients do not contribute because they minimize the quadratic form.
The concentrated log-likelihood is
`-(N/2)(log(2 pi sigma^2) + 1) - (1/2) log det R`.

- Domain: `log ell_i in [log(0.01 E_i), log(20 E_i)]` with `E_i` the extent
  of input `i`; an estimated nugget in `[nugget, 1]` (computational choices).
- Meaning: maximum likelihood with `beta` and `sigma^2` profiled out; BFGS
  with the exact gradient and seeded multi-starts.
- Code: `surrogates.fit_universal_kriging`, `surrogates.UniversalKriging`.

### EQ-SUR-UK-004 — Closed-form kriging leave-one-out

`B = [[sigma^2 R, F], [F', 0]]^-1`; `mu_(-i) = Y_i - sum_j (B_ij / B_ii) Y_j`,
`s^2_(-i) = 1 / B_ii`; with `P = R^-1 - R^-1 F (F'R^-1F)^-1 F'R^-1` the
top-left block of `B` is `P / sigma^2` and `P Y = alpha`, so
`mu_(-i) = Y_i - alpha_i / P_ii` and `s^2_(-i) = sigma^2 / P_ii`;
`epsilon_LOO = (1/N) sum_i (Y_i - mu_(-i))^2 / Var(Y)`.

- Domain: fixed length scales and `sigma^2`; `beta` is re-estimated in every
  fold.
- Meaning: cross-validation without refitting; the error used to choose the
  trend of optimal PC-Kriging.
- Code: `surrogates.UniversalKriging.leave_one_out`,
  `surrogates.UniversalKriging.loo_error`, `surrogates.PCKriging.loo_error`.

### EQ-SUR-PCK-001 — Sequential and optimal PC-Kriging

`M(x) ~= sum_(alpha in A) beta_alpha Psi_alpha(xi(x)) + sigma^2 Z(xi(x))`:
universal kriging [EQ-SUR-UK-001] with orthonormal polynomial regressors.
Sequential: `A` is the set chosen by hybrid LAR [EQ-SUR-SPCE-004]. Optimal:
with the LAR order `alpha_1, alpha_2, ...` of that set,
`A* = argmin_k epsilon_LOO({0, alpha_1, ..., alpha_k})` [EQ-SUR-UK-004].

- Domain: as [EQ-SUR-UK-001]; `xi(x)` the standardized PCE variables, used
  for the polynomials and the correlation alike.
- Meaning: the polynomials capture the global behaviour and the process the
  local residual. The full trend is fitted with all optimizer starts,
  exactly as the sequential model, so the optimal error is never larger;
  each smaller trend starts from the optimum of the previous size.
- Code: `surrogates.fit_pc_kriging`, `surrogates.pc_kriging_from_samples`,
  `surrogates.PCKriging`, `surrogates.ChaosTrend`.

### EQ-SUR-PCE-001 — Polynomial chaos expansion

`Y ~= sum_(alpha in A) c_alpha Psi_alpha(xi)`,
`Psi_alpha(xi) = prod_i psi_(alpha_i)(xi_i)`, with
`E[Psi_alpha Psi_beta] = delta_(alpha beta)` under independent `xi`.
`xi = u` (Nataf standard normals, [EQ-MOD-NATAF-001]) for the Hermite basis;
`xi_i = (2 x_i - a_i - b_i)/(b_i - a_i)` for independent `Uniform(a_i,b_i)`
inputs and the Legendre basis.

- Domain: square-integrable output; Legendre requires independent uniform
  inputs.
- Meaning: spectral surrogate whose coefficients encode moments and variance
  decomposition.
- Code: `surrogates.PolynomialChaosExpansion.predict`,
  `surrogates.PolynomialChaosExpansion.basis_values`.

### EQ-SUR-PCE-004 — Total-degree and hyperbolic truncation

`A_(p,q) = {alpha in N^d : (sum_i alpha_i^q)^(1/q) <= p}`, `0 < q <= 1`;
`|A_(p,1)| = C(d+p, p)`.

- Domain: `d >= 1`, `p >= 0`.
- Meaning: `q = 1` keeps all terms up to total degree `p`; `q < 1` drops
  high-order interactions first (sparsity-of-effects).

### EQ-SUR-PCE-005 — Least-squares PCE coefficients

`c = argmin sum_j (y_j - Psi(x_j)'c)^2 = (Psi' Psi)^-1 Psi' y`, solved as
`c = R^-1 Q' y` from a Householder QR factorization `Psi = Q R` (the
information matrix `Psi' Psi` is never formed for the solve).

- Domain: more design points than basis terms and a positive-definite
  information matrix; `Psi' Psi / N` near the identity indicates a
  well-conditioned design.
- Meaning: non-intrusive regression fit on an experimental design drawn from
  the input law.
- Code: `surrogates.polynomial_chaos_from_samples`,
  `surrogates.fit_polynomial_chaos`, `surrogates.normalized_information_matrix`.
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011] and [REF-SUDRET-2008].

### EQ-SUR-PCE-006 — Closed-form leave-one-out error

`Delta_j = (y_j - y_hat_j)/(1 - h_j)`, `h_j = psi_j' (Psi' Psi)^-1 psi_j`;
`epsilon_LOO = N^-1 sum_j Delta_j^2 / Var_hat(y)`.

- Domain: [EQ-SUR-PCE-005] with `h_j < 1`.
- Meaning: generalization-error estimate from one fit, no extra model runs.
- Code: `surrogates.PolynomialChaosExpansion.loo_error`.
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011].
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-PCE-006](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-pce-006--closed-form-leave-one-out-error)

### EQ-SUR-PCE-007 — PCE moments

`E[Y] = c_0`, `Var[Y] = sum_(alpha != 0) c_alpha^2`.

### EQ-SUR-PCE-008 — Sobol indices from PCE coefficients

`S_u = sum_(alpha in A_u) c_alpha^2 / Var[Y]` with

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-003 — Corrected leave-one-out error

`epsilon*_LOO = T(P, N) epsilon_LOO` with
`T(P, N) = N/(N-P) (1 + tr((Psi'Psi)^-1)) = N/(N-P) (1 + tr(C^-1)/N)`,
`C = Psi'Psi / N`, and `tr((Psi'Psi)^-1) = ||R^-1||_F^2` for `Psi = Q R`.

- Domain: `N > P`, full-rank `Psi`; `epsilon_LOO` from [EQ-SUR-PCE-006].
- Meaning: the plain LOO error is optimistic when `P` is not small next to
  `N`; for an orthonormal design `tr(C^-1) ~= P` and `T ~= (N+P)/(N-P)`.
- Code: `surrogates.PolynomialChaosExpansion.corrected_loo_error`,
  `surrogates.polynomial_chaos_from_samples`,
  `surrogates.SparseChaosSelection.loo_path`.
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011] and [REF-CHAPELLE-VAPNIK-BENGIO-2002].
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.

"Model Selection for Small Sample Regression." *Machine Learning* 48(1-3), 9-23, 2002. - Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-003--corrected-leave-one-out-error)

## `probstat.surrogates.sparse_chaos`

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-001 — Least-angle regression step

On centred unit-norm columns `X` with correlations `c = X'(y - mu)`,
`C = max_j |c_j|`, active set `A` and signs `s_j = sign(c_j)`:
`G_A = X_A' X_A`, `A_A = (s' G_A^-1 s)^(-1/2)`,
`u_A = A_A X_A G_A^-1 s`, `a = X' u_A`, and `mu <- mu + gamma u_A` with
`gamma = min+_(j not in A) {(C - c_j)/(A_A - a_j), (C + c_j)/(A_A + a_j)}`,
or `gamma = C / A_A` (least squares on `A`) when no column may enter.

- Domain: `N >= 2` rows; columns constant on the rows, or numerically in
  the span of the active ones, never enter; at most `min(m, N - 1)` steps.
- Meaning: every active column keeps the same absolute correlation with
  the residual, which falls until an inactive column ties; the entry order
  ranks the regressors. Centring absorbs the intercept (the constant PCE
  term, which is always kept and not ranked).
- Code: `surrogates.least_angle_regression`,
  `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples`.
- Source: [REF-EFRON-HASTIE-JOHNSTONE-TIBSHIRANI-2004].
  - [REF-EFRON-HASTIE-JOHNSTONE-TIBSHIRANI-2004](../references.md#ref-efron-hastie-johnstone-tibshirani-2004): Bradley Efron, Trevor Hastie, Iain Johnstone, and Robert Tibshirani. "Least Angle Regression." *The Annals of Statistics* 32(2), 407-499, 2004. The least-angle regression path: equiangular steps between the knots where a new regressor becomes as correlated with the residual as the active ones. DOI: [10.1214/009053604000000067](https://doi.org/10.1214/009053604000000067).
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-001--least-angle-regression-step)

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-002 — Orthogonal matching pursuit selection

`j_(k+1) = argmax_(j not in A_k) |psi_j' r_k| / ||psi_j - mean(psi_j)||`,
`r_k = y - Psi_(A_k) c_(A_k)` the least-squares residual on the constant
and the `k` terms selected so far.

- Domain: as [EQ-SUR-SPCE-001]; the residual is recomputed by least squares
  after every addition.
- Meaning: greedy forward selection of the term most correlated with what
  the current fit misses; the nested sets are then scored by
  [EQ-SUR-SPCE-003] exactly as for LAR. It fits the training runs more
  aggressively than LAR, so its corrected LOO error is more optimistic.
- Code: `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples` (`method="omp"`).
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011] for the LOO-based choice of the nested
  set; the selection rule is the standard orthogonal matching pursuit.
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-002--orthogonal-matching-pursuit-selection)

### EQ-SUR-SPCE-004 — Hybrid LAR selection over degrees and q-norms

`(p*, q*, k*) = argmin epsilon*_LOO(A_k(p, q))` over the requested degrees
`p`, norms `q`, and steps `k`, where `A_k(p, q)` is the constant plus the
first `k` terms ranked by [EQ-SUR-SPCE-001] (or [EQ-SUR-SPCE-002]) on the
truncation set `A_(p,q)` of [EQ-SUR-PCE-004], each refitted by
[EQ-SUR-PCE-005].

- Domain: `N >= 3`; `k <= N - 2`, so the set may be larger than the design.
- Meaning: LAR only ranks the terms; ordinary least squares on each nested
  set removes the LAR shrinkage, and the corrected LOO error chooses the
  sparsity, the degree, and the norm. Early stopping (a computational
  choice) cuts a path after `max(10, ceil(0.1 k_max))` steps without
  improvement and the degree loop after two degrees without improvement.
- Code: `surrogates.sparse_polynomial_chaos_from_samples`,
  `surrogates.fit_sparse_polynomial_chaos`,
  `surrogates.SparseChaosSelection`.
- Source: [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011].
  - [REF-BLATMAN-SUDRET-2011](../references.md#ref-blatman-sudret-2011): Géraud Blatman and Bruno Sudret. "Adaptive Sparse Polynomial Chaos Expansion Based on Least Angle Regression." *Journal of Computational Physics* 230(6), 2345-2367, 2011.
- Registry: [EQ-SUR-SPCE-004](../equation-registry.md#eq-sur-spce-004--hybrid-lar-selection-over-degrees-and-q-norms)
