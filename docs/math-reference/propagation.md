## `probstat.propagation.margins`

### EQ-PROP-MARGIN-001 — Margin, uncertainty, and confidence ratio

Upper limit `L`: `M = L - y*`, `U = y_bound - y*`. Lower limit: `M = y* - L`,
`U = y* - y_bound`. Confidence ratio `M / U` (``+inf`` for `M > 0`, `U <= 0`).

- Domain: finite best estimate `y*`; `y_bound` a stated bound of the
  performance on the unsafe side, for example a Wilks or normal tolerance
  bound of the `p`-quantile at confidence `gamma`; a band applies each side
  separately and the side with the smallest ratio binds.
- Meaning: the margin is the room between the estimate and the limit, the
  uncertainty the room that the stated bound of the performance uses; a
  ratio above one means the margin covers the bound. For nested
  aleatory-epistemic studies `U = U_aleatory + U_epistemic` with
  `U_aleatory` taken at the most favourable outer point.

  `propagation.quantify_margin_mixed`,
  `propagation.TechnicalPerformanceMeasure`.

## `probstat.propagation.mixed`

### EQ-PROP-MIXED-001 — Nested (double-loop) propagation

For outer epistemic values `e_j` (Latin hypercube over the epistemic
dimensions, plus interval corners and optional compass-search points):
`F_j(y) = (1/n) sum_i 1{g(x_i, e_j) <= y}` with `x_i` the aleatory inner
sample (common to every `j` with common random numbers); statistics
`s(e_j)` = mean, `P(Y > t)`, type-7 quantiles; bounds
`[min_j s(e_j), max_j s(e_j)]`.

- Domain: aleatory and epistemic inputs independent; at least one of each.
- Meaning: separates variability (inner CDF) from lack of knowledge (outer
  spread). The bounds over a finite design are inner approximations of the
  true bounds (exact at the corners for responses monotone in each interval
  input); cost `(outer points) x n` model evaluations.
- Code: `propagation.nested_propagation`.
- Source: [REF-ROY-OBERKAMPF-2011], [REF-OBERKAMPF-ROY-2010].

### EQ-PROP-PBOX-001 — Probability box from a family of CDFs

`F_low(y) = min_j F_j(y)`, `F_up(y) = max_j F_j(y)`;
`P(Y > t)` in `[1 - F_up(t), 1 - F_low(t)]`.

- Domain: the outer points of [EQ-PROP-MIXED-001].
- Meaning: the pointwise envelope of the output CDFs (a p-box); for

## `probstat.propagation.study`

### EQ-PROP-QCI-001 — Distribution-free order-statistic interval for a quantile

`P(Y_(l) <= xi_p <= Y_(u)) = B(u - 1) - B(l - 1)` with `B` the
`Binomial(n, p)` CDF; `l = max{k >= 1 : B(k - 1) <= alpha/2}`,
`u = min{k <= n : B(k - 1) >= 1 - alpha/2}`.

- Domain: `n` independent draws of a continuous law, `0 < p < 1`; undefined
  (error) when no rank pair reaches the confidence.
- Meaning: an exact, law-free confidence interval for the `p`-quantile whose
  coverage is at least `1 - alpha`, with each tail at most `alpha/2`; for LHS
  or quasi-random samples it is a conservative reference only.
- Code: `propagation.order_statistic_interval`,
  `propagation.OutputDistribution.quantile_interval`.
- Source: [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003, Section 7.1].
  - [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003](../references.md#ref-david-nagaraja-2003): H. A. David and H. N. Nagaraja. *Order Statistics*. Third edition. Wiley Series in Probability and Statistics. John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken, NJ, 2003.
- Registry: [EQ-PROP-QCI-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-prop-qci-001--distribution-free-order-statistic-interval-for-a-quantile)

### EQ-PROP-RQMC-CI-001 — Student-t interval over randomized replicates

`theta_hat = (1/r) sum_k theta_k`, `s_r^2 = sum_k (theta_k - theta_hat)^2 /
(r - 1)`, interval `theta_hat +- t_{1 - alpha/2, r - 1} s_r / sqrt(r)`.

- Domain: `r >= 2` independent randomizations (scrambles, shifts, or
  independent Monte Carlo batches) of one design; `theta_k` the replicate
  mean or exceedance fraction.
- Meaning: the honest error statement of randomized quasi-Monte Carlo; the
  replicate estimates are i.i.d. and unbiased, and the t quantile is accurate
  when their distribution is near normal.
- Code: `propagation.replicate_interval`,
  `propagation.OutputDistribution.mean_interval`,
  `propagation.OutputDistribution.exceedance_interval`.
- Source: [REF-L-ECUYER-2018] and [REF-OWEN-2013, Chapter 17].
  - [REF-L-ECUYER-2018](../references.md#ref-l-ecuyer-2018): Pierre L'Ecuyer. "Randomized Quasi-Monte Carlo: An Introduction for Practitioners." In *Monte Carlo and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods (MCQMC 2016)*, Springer Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics 241, 29-52, 2018.

### EQ-PROP-VARCI-001 — Chi-square interval for a normal variance

`[(n - 1) s^2 / chi2_{1 - alpha/2, n - 1}, (n - 1) s^2 / chi2_{alpha/2, n - 1}]`.

- Domain: `n >= 2` independent normal observations with sample variance
  `s^2`.
- Meaning: exact for normal data because `(n - 1) s^2 / sigma^2 ~
  chi2_{n - 1}`; not robust: for non-normal data its coverage depends on the
  kurtosis even as `n` grows, so the percentile bootstrap
  ([EQ-INF-BOOT-002]) is offered as the alternative.
- Code: `propagation.OutputDistribution.variance_interval`.
- Source: [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002, Chapter 9].
  - [REF-CASELLA-BERGER-2002](../references.md#ref-casella-berger-2002): George Casella and Roger L. Berger. *Statistical Inference*. 2nd edition. Duxbury, 2002.

### EQ-PROP-TOL-001 — One-sided exact normal tolerance factor

`k(n, p, gamma) = t'_(gamma; n-1, delta) / sqrt(n)` with `delta = z_p sqrt(n)`;
the bound is `xbar + k s` (upper) or `xbar - k s` (lower).

- Domain: `n >= 2` independent draws of a normal law, `0 < p < 1`,
  `0 < gamma < 1`; `t'_(gamma; nu, delta)` is the `gamma`-quantile of the
  noncentral `t` law with `nu` degrees of freedom and noncentrality `delta`.
- Meaning: `(xbar - mu - z_p sigma) / (s / sqrt(n))` is `T' - delta` with
  `T'` noncentral `t`, so `P(xbar + k s >= mu + z_p sigma) = gamma` exactly:
  the bound exceeds the `p`-quantile with probability `gamma`.

### EQ-PROP-TOL-002 — Noncentral t distribution function as a chi integral

`P(T' <= t) = int_0^inf Phi(t s - delta) f_S(s) ds`, with
`f_S(s) = 2 nu s f_V(nu s^2)` the density of `S = sqrt(V / nu)` for
`V ~ chi2(nu)`.

- Domain: `nu >= 1` integer, `delta` and `t` real.
- Meaning: given `S = s`, `T' = (Z + delta) / s` is below `t` exactly when
  `Z <= t s - delta` with `Z` standard normal, so the distribution function
  is the average of `Phi(t s - delta)` over the law of `S`. The integral is
  truncated where `V` has `1e-17` of its mass on each side.
- Code: `propagation.tolerance._noncentral_t_cdf`.

### EQ-PROP-TOL-003 — Two-sided exact normal tolerance factor

`2 int_0^inf phi(z) P(chi2_nu >= nu r(z)^2 / k^2) dz = gamma`, `nu = n - 1`,
where `r(z)` solves `Phi(z / sqrt(n) + r) - Phi(z / sqrt(n) - r) = p`.

- Domain: `n >= 2`, `0 < p < 1 - 1e-10`, `0 < gamma < 1`.
- Meaning: write `xbar = mu + sigma z / sqrt(n)` and `s = sigma S`. The
  content of `[xbar - k s, xbar + k s]` is
  `Phi(z / sqrt(n) + k S) - Phi(z / sqrt(n) - k S)`, increasing in `k S`, so
  it reaches `p` exactly when `k S >= r(z)`. Averaging over `z ~ N(0, 1)`
  and the chi law of `S` gives the probability that the interval holds at
  least `p` of the law; its root in `k` is the exact two-sided factor.
- Code: `propagation.normal_tolerance_factor`,
  `propagation.normal_tolerance_interval`,
  `propagation.tolerance._two_sided_exact_factor`.

### EQ-PROP-TOL-004 — Howe approximation of the two-sided factor

`k_Howe = z_((1+p)/2) sqrt(nu (1 + 1/n) / chi2_(1-gamma; nu))`, `nu = n - 1`,
with `chi2_(1-gamma; nu)` the `(1 - gamma)`-quantile of the chi-square law.

- Domain: `n >= 2`, `0 < p < 1`, `0 < gamma < 1`.
- Meaning: a closed form that replaces the sample-mean deviation by its
  scale `sqrt(1 + 1/n)`. At `p = gamma = 0.95` it is 2.1 % above the exact
  factor at `n = 3` and 0.3 % below it at `n = 10`, so the stated confidence
  is slightly missed for moderate `n`.
- Code: `propagation.normal_tolerance_factor` (`method="howe"`),
  `propagation.tolerance._howe_factor`.

## `probstat.propagation.wilks`

### EQ-PROP-WILKS-001 — Wilks one-sided order-statistic bound of a quantile

`P(Y_(k) >= q_a) = P(Binomial(n, a) <= k - 1) = sum_(j=0)^(k-1) C(n, j) a^j (1 - a)^(n - j)`;
the upper Wilks rank is the smallest `k` with this probability at least
`beta`. Lower bound: `P(Y_(k) <= q_a) = P(Binomial(n, a) >= k)`, largest `k`
with this probability at least `beta`.

- Domain: `n` independent draws of one law, `0 < a < 1`, `0 < beta < 1`;
  exact for a continuous law, conservative (confidence at least `beta`) for
  a law with atoms; undefined (error) when even `Y_(n)` (or `Y_(1)`) falls
  short.
- Meaning: the number of draws below `q_a` is `Binomial(n, a)` whatever the
  law, so a fixed order statistic bounds the quantile with a known,
  law-free confidence; the whole risk `1 - beta` sits on one side, unlike
  the equal-tailed interval of EQ-PROP-QCI-001. With `n = 1000` and
  `a = beta = 0.95` the upper rank is `k = 962`.
- Code: `propagation.wilks_rank`, `propagation.wilks_bound`,
  `propagation.wilks._upper_confidence`,
  `propagation.wilks._lower_confidence`.
- Source: [REF-WILKS-1941], [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003, Section 7.1].
  - [REF-WILKS-1941](../references.md#ref-wilks-1941): S. S. Wilks. "Determination of Sample Sizes for Setting Tolerance Limits." *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 12(1), 91-96, 1941. The binomial order-statistic argument behind distribution-free quantile bounds and the 59-run 95 %/95 % sample size. DOI: [10.1214/aoms/1177731788](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731788).
  - [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003](../references.md#ref-david-nagaraja-2003): H. A. David and H. N. Nagaraja. *Order Statistics*. Third edition. Wiley Series in Probability and Statistics. John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken, NJ, 2003.

### EQ-PROP-WILKS-002 — Wilks sample size for an order-m quantile bound

Upper bound by the `m`-th largest value: smallest `n` with
`P(Binomial(n, a) <= n - m) >= beta`; for `m = 1`,
`n = ceil(ln(1 - beta) / ln a)`. Lower bound by the `m`-th smallest value:
smallest `n` with `P(Binomial(n, a) >= m) >= beta`.

- Domain: `0 < a < 1`, `0 < beta < 1`, integer `m >= 1`; independent draws
  of a continuous law.
- Meaning: the number of code runs needed before the `m`-th extreme value is
  a `beta`-confidence bound of the `a`-quantile; `a = beta = 0.95` gives 59,
  93, 124, and 153 runs for `m = 1..4`, and the median needs 5 runs at
  `beta = 0.95`. The upper case equals the success-run sample size with
  `m - 1` allowed failures (EQ-REL-DATA-004).
- Code: `propagation.wilks_sample_size`.
- Source: [REF-WILKS-1941], [REF-NUTT-WALLIS-2004].
  - [REF-WILKS-1941](../references.md#ref-wilks-1941): S. S. Wilks. "Determination of Sample Sizes for Setting Tolerance Limits." *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 12(1), 91-96, 1941. The binomial order-statistic argument behind distribution-free quantile bounds and the 59-run 95 %/95 % sample size. DOI: [10.1214/aoms/1177731788](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731788).
  - [REF-NUTT-WALLIS-2004](../references.md#ref-nutt-wallis-2004): William T. Nutt and Graham B. Wallis. "Evaluation of Nuclear Safety from the Outputs of Computer Codes in the Presence of Uncertainties." *Reliability Engineering and System Safety* 83, 57-77, 2004. Higher-order Wilks sample sizes (59, 93, 124, 153 runs at 95 %/95 %) in best-estimate-plus-uncertainty safety analysis.

### EQ-PROP-WILKS-003 — Two-sided distribution-free tolerance interval

The content `C = F(Y_(n-m+1)) - F(Y_(m))` of `[Y_(m), Y_(n-m+1)]` follows
`Beta(n - 2m + 1, 2m)`, so
`P(C >= gamma) = 1 - I_gamma(n - 2m + 1, 2m) = P(Binomial(n, gamma) <= n - 2m)`.

- Domain: `n` independent draws of a continuous law `F`, `0 < gamma < 1`,
  `0 < beta < 1`, `1 <= m <= n/2`.
- Meaning: the interval between the `m`-th smallest and `m`-th largest
  values contains at least a fraction `gamma` of the law with the stated
  probability; a two-sided order `m` costs as many runs as a one-sided order
  `2m` (93 runs for `gamma = beta = 0.95`, `m = 1`).
- Code: `propagation.wilks_tolerance_interval`,
  `propagation.wilks_sample_size`.
- Source: [REF-WILKS-1942], [REF-DAVID-NAGARAJA-2003, Section 7.2].
  - [REF-WILKS-1942](../references.md#ref-wilks-1942): S. S. Wilks. "Statistical Prediction with Special Reference to the Problem of Tolerance Limits." *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 13(4), 400-409, 1942. Two-sided distribution-free tolerance intervals and the Beta law of their content. DOI: [10.1214/aoms/1177731537](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731537).
