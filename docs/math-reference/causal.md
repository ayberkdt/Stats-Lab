### EQ-CAUSAL-DID-001 — Canonical 2x2 difference-in-differences

`DiD = (Ybar_{1,post} - Ybar_{1,pre}) - (Ybar_{0,post} - Ybar_{0,pre})
= mean(d_i | treated) - mean(d_i | control)`, `d_i = Y_i,post - Y_i,pre`,
`se = sqrt(s_1^2 / n_1 + s_0^2 / n_0)` with the sample variances of `d_i`.

- Domain: two periods, a balanced panel, at least two units per group.
- Meaning: the ATT under parallel trends and no anticipation; the standard
  error assumes independent units.
- Code: `causal.did_two_by_two`.
- Source: [REF-ROTH-ET-AL-2023].

A Synthesis of the Recent Econometrics Literature." *Journal of Econometrics* 235(2), 2218-2244, 2023. ### EQ-CAUSAL-DID-002 — Group-time average treatment effect

`ATT(g, t) = E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} | G = g] - E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} | C]`, `t >= g`,
estimated by sample means, with `C = {G = inf}` (never treated) or
`C = {G > t}` (not yet treated, including never treated).

- Domain: balanced panel, absorbing treatment, `g` later than the first
  period, a non-empty comparison set; no covariates.
- Meaning: effect at period `t` for units first treated at `g`, under
  parallel trends between the group and the comparison units and no
  anticipation.
- Code: `causal.group_time_effects`, `causal.GroupTimeEffects.effect`.

### EQ-CAUSAL-DID-003 — Influence-function standard errors

For a cell with long differences `d_i`, group mean `mu_g` and comparison
mean `mu_C`: `psi_i = (n / n_g) 1{G_i = g}(d_i - mu_g) - (n / n_C)
1{i in C}(d_i - mu_C)`, `se = sqrt(sum_i psi_i^2) / n`. For an aggregate
`theta = sum_c w_c ATT_c` with `w_c = n_{g(c)} / sum_{c'} n_{g(c')}`:
`psi_i = sum_c w_c psi_{c,i} + sum_c (ATT_c - theta) 1{G_i = g(c)} / P`,
`P = sum_c n_{g(c)} / n`.

- Domain: independent units; fixed number of periods; at least two units
  in the group and in the comparison set (otherwise the standard error is
  `nan`).
- Meaning: asymptotic standard errors; the second term of the aggregate is
  the delta method for the estimated group shares. Pointwise, not
  simultaneous. Too small for small arms (divisor-`n` variances and a
  normal critical value): 86 to 91 % coverage of nominal 95 % intervals
  with 5 never-treated units among 100.
- Code: `causal.group_time_effects`, `causal.GroupTimeEffects.event_study`,
  `causal.GroupTimeEffects.overall`.
- Source: [REF-CALLAWAY-SANTANNA-2021] (influence-function approach); the
  unconditional forms are derived here and checked by Monte Carlo.
  - [REF-CALLAWAY-SANTANNA-2021](../references.md#ref-callaway-santanna-2021): Brantly Callaway and Pedro H. C. Sant'Anna. "Difference-in-Differences with Multiple Time Periods." *Journal of Econometrics* 225(2), 200-230, 2021. Group-time average treatment effects for staggered adoption with never-treated or not-yet-treated comparison units, and their aggregation into event-study and overall summaries. DOI: [10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-DID-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-did-003--influence-function-standard-errors)

### EQ-CAUSAL-ADJ-001 — Backdoor adjustment formula

`P(y | do(x)) = sum_z P(y | x, z) P(z)`.

- Domain: `Z` satisfies [EQ-CAUSAL-BD-001]; positivity `P(x, z) > 0`
  whenever `P(z) > 0`.
- Meaning: standardize the stratum-specific outcome law to the population
  distribution of `Z` instead of its distribution among the treated. The
  naive `P(y | x) = sum_z P(y | x, z) P(z | x)` can reverse sign (Simpson).
- Code: `causal.backdoor_adjustment`, `causal.compare_naive_and_adjusted`.

### EQ-CAUSAL-FD-001 — Frontdoor adjustment formula

`P(y | do(x)) = sum_m P(m | x) sum_x' P(y | x', m) P(x')`.

- Domain: `M` satisfies [EQ-CAUSAL-FDC-001]; `P(x) > 0` and `P(x', m) > 0`
  whenever `P(m | x) > 0` and `P(x') > 0`.
- Meaning: identifies the effect despite an unobserved treatment-outcome
  confounder by chaining two unconfounded steps.
- Code: `causal.frontdoor_adjustment`.

## `probstat.causal.double_ml`

### EQ-CAUSAL-DML-001 — Partialling-out estimator of the partially linear model

For `Y = theta D + g(X) + e`, `D = m(X) + v`, `l(X) = E[Y | X]`: residuals
`Y~ = Y - l_hat(X)`, `D~ = D - m_hat(X)` with `l_hat`, `m_hat` fitted on the
folds not containing the unit, and `theta_hat = sum(D~ Y~) / sum(D~^2)`,
the root of the pooled score `sum (Y~ - theta D~) D~ = 0`.

- Domain: `sum(D~^2) > 0`; `K >= 2` folds for cross-fitting (`K = 1` uses
  in-sample fits).
- Meaning: the score is Neyman orthogonal (its derivative with respect to
  `(l, m)` vanishes at the truth), so nuisance errors enter only through
  their product; with linear in-sample nuisances it is the least-squares
  coefficient of `D` (Frisch-Waugh-Lovell).
- Code: `causal.dml_partially_linear`.
- Source: [REF-CHERNOZHUKOV-ET-AL-2018].
  - [REF-CHERNOZHUKOV-ET-AL-2018](../references.md#ref-chernozhukov-et-al-2018): Victor Chernozhukov, Denis Chetverikov, Mert Demirer, Esther Duflo, Christian Hansen, Whitney Newey, and James Robins. "Double/Debiased Machine Learning for Treatment and Structural Parameters." *The Econometrics Journal* 21(1), C1-C68, 2018. Neyman-orthogonal scores combined with cross-fitting give root-n consistent, asymptotically normal estimates of a low-dimensional parameter when the nuisance functions are estimated by machine-learning methods that converge slowly. DOI: [10.1111/ectj.12097](https://doi.org/10.1111/ectj.12097).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-DML-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-dml-001--partialling-out-estimator-of-the-partially-linear-model)

### EQ-CAUSAL-DML-002 — Asymptotic variance of the partialling-out estimator

### EQ-CAUSAL-PS-001 — Logistic propensity score

### EQ-CAUSAL-IPW-001 — Inverse-probability-weighted ATE

Horvitz-Thompson: `tau_HT = (1/n) sum_i [T_i Y_i / e_i - (1 - T_i) Y_i /
(1 - e_i)]`. Hajek: `tau_H = sum T Y / e / sum T / e - sum (1-T) Y / (1-e) /
sum (1-T) / (1-e)`. Standard error `sd(psi_i) / sqrt(n)` with the
corresponding influence values, treating `e` as known. Trimming keeps units
with `a <= e_i <= 1 - a`.

- Domain: `0 < e_i < 1`; after trimming the estimand is the ATE of the
  overlap population.
- Meaning: reweighting creates a pseudo-population in which `T` is
  independent of `X`.
- Code: `causal.estimate_ipw`.
- Source: [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020, Chapter 12],
  [REF-IMBENS-RUBIN-2015, Chapter 16].
  - [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020](../references.md#ref-hernan-robins-2020): Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins. *Causal Inference: What If*. Chapman and Hall/CRC, 2020.

### EQ-CAUSAL-SW-001 — Stabilized weights

`SW_i = T_i p / e_i + (1 - T_i)(1 - p) / (1 - e_i)` with `p = mean(T)`; the

- Domain: [EQ-CAUSAL-IPW-001].

## `probstat.causal.graphs`

### EQ-CAUSAL-DSEP-001 — d-separation via the moralized ancestral graph

`X _||_ Y | Z` in a DAG `G` iff `Z` separates `X` from `Y` in the undirected
moral graph `(G_An(X u Y u Z))^m`: restrict `G` to the ancestral set of
`X u Y u Z`, join every pair of parents of a common child, drop directions,
and delete `Z`.

- Domain: finite DAG; `X`, `Y` non-empty; `X`, `Y`, `Z` pairwise disjoint.
- Meaning: every path between `X` and `Y` is blocked by `Z` (a non-collider
  in `Z`, or a collider with neither itself nor a descendant in `Z`); then the
  conditional independence holds in every distribution Markov to `G`.
- Code: `causal.CausalGraph.is_d_separated`.
- Source: [REF-PEARL-2009, Section 1.2.3], [REF-LAURITZEN-ETAL-1990].
  - [REF-PEARL-2009](../references.md#ref-pearl-2009): Judea Pearl. *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*. 2nd edition. Cambridge University Press, 2009.
  - [REF-LAURITZEN-ETAL-1990](../references.md#ref-lauritzen-etal-1990): S. L. Lauritzen, A. P. Dawid, B. N. Larsen, and H.-G. Leimer. "Independence Properties of Directed Markov Fields." *Networks* 20(5), 491-505, 1990.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-DSEP-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-dsep-001--d-separation-via-the-moralized-ancestral-graph)

### EQ-CAUSAL-BD-001 — Backdoor criterion

`Z` satisfies the backdoor criterion relative to `(X, Y)` iff
`Z n De(X) = {}` and `X _||_ Y | Z` in `G_underline(X)` (the graph with all
edges leaving `X` removed). Every minimal such `Z` lies in
`An_{G_underline(X)}(X u Y)`.

- Domain: [EQ-CAUSAL-DSEP-001]; `Z` disjoint from `X u Y`.
- Meaning: `Z` blocks every path into `X` that could carry confounding and
  opens no new path; then [EQ-CAUSAL-ADJ-001] identifies `P(y | do(x))`.
- Code: `causal.satisfies_backdoor`, `causal.minimal_adjustment_sets`.
- Source: [REF-PEARL-2009, Definition 3.3.1 and Theorem 3.3.2],
  [REF-LAURITZEN-ETAL-1990].
  - [REF-PEARL-2009](../references.md#ref-pearl-2009): Judea Pearl. *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*. 2nd edition. Cambridge University Press, 2009.
  - [REF-LAURITZEN-ETAL-1990](../references.md#ref-lauritzen-etal-1990): S. L. Lauritzen, A. P. Dawid, B. N. Larsen, and H.-G. Leimer. "Independence Properties of Directed Markov Fields." *Networks* 20(5), 491-505, 1990.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-BD-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-bd-001--backdoor-criterion)

### EQ-CAUSAL-FDC-001 — Frontdoor criterion

`M` satisfies the frontdoor criterion relative to `(X, Y)` iff (i) every
directed path `X -> ... -> Y` meets `M`; (ii) `X _||_ M` in
`G_underline(X)`; (iii) `M _||_ Y | X` in `G_underline(M)`.

- Domain: single `X` and `Y`; non-empty `M` disjoint from `{X, Y}`.
- Meaning: the effect is transmitted only through `M`, `X -> M` is
  unconfounded, and `M -> Y` is deconfounded by `X`; then
  [EQ-CAUSAL-FD-001] identifies `P(y | do(x))`.
- Code: `causal.satisfies_frontdoor`.
- Source: [REF-PEARL-2009, Definition 3.3.3 and Theorem 3.3.4].
  - [REF-PEARL-2009](../references.md#ref-pearl-2009): Judea Pearl. *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*. 2nd edition. Cambridge University Press, 2009.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-FDC-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-fdc-001--frontdoor-criterion)

## `probstat.causal.instrumental`

### EQ-CAUSAL-IV-001 — Two-stage least squares

With `X = [D, W]` (endogenous and included exogenous regressors) and
`Zf = [Z, W]`: `Xhat = Zf (Zf'Zf)^-1 Zf'X`,
`beta = (Xhat'X)^-1 Xhat'y = (Xhat'Xhat)^-1 Xhat'y`. Exactly identified:
`beta = (Zf'X)^-1 Zf'y`; one binary instrument and an intercept: the Wald
ratio `(E[Y|Z=1] - E[Y|Z=0]) / (E[D|Z=1] - E[D|Z=0])`.

- Domain: at least as many excluded instruments as endogenous regressors;
  `Zf` and `Xhat` of full column rank.
- Meaning: consistent (not unbiased) for the structural coefficients under
  instrument relevance, exclusion and exchangeability with homogeneous
  effects.
- Code: `causal.two_stage_least_squares`.
- Source: [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020, Chapter 16].
  - [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020](../references.md#ref-hernan-robins-2020): Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins. *Causal Inference: What If*. Chapman and Hall/CRC, 2020.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-IV-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-iv-001--two-stage-least-squares)

### EQ-CAUSAL-IV-002 — Heteroskedasticity-robust covariance of 2SLS

`Cov(beta) = (Xhat'Xhat)^-1 (sum_i e_i^2 xhat_i xhat_i') (Xhat'Xhat)^-1`,
`e = y - X beta` (observed regressors); `HC1` multiplies by `n / (n - K)`.

- Domain: [EQ-CAUSAL-IV-001].
- Meaning: sandwich covariance of the moment condition `E[xhat u] = 0`;
  asymptotic, and unreliable with weak instruments. In the exactly
  identified case it equals `(Zf'X)^-1 (sum e_i^2 z_i z_i') (X'Zf)^-1`.
- Code: `causal.two_stage_least_squares`,
  `causal.InstrumentalVariablesResult.confidence_interval`.
- Source: [REF-WHITE-1980] (heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance),
  applied to the second-stage regressors.
  - [REF-WHITE-1980](../references.md#ref-white-1980): Halbert White. "A Heteroskedasticity-Consistent Covariance Matrix Estimator and a Direct Test for Heteroskedasticity." *Econometrica* 48(4), 817-838, 1980.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-IV-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-iv-002--heteroskedasticity-robust-covariance-of-2sls)

### EQ-CAUSAL-IV-003 — First-stage F statistic of the excluded instruments

`F = ((RSS_W - RSS_Zf) / q) / (RSS_Zf / (n - q - k_W))` for the regression
of an endogenous regressor on `Zf = [Z, W]` against its regression on `W`.

- Domain: `n > q + k_W`.
- Meaning: strength of the instruments; values below about 10 are a common
  rule-of-thumb warning of weak instruments (a heuristic).
- Code: `causal.two_stage_least_squares`.
- Source: [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020, Chapter 16],
  [REF-MONTGOMERY-PECK-VINING-2021, Chapter 3] (extra-sum-of-squares F).
  - [REF-HERNAN-ROBINS-2020](../references.md#ref-hernan-robins-2020): Miguel A. Hernán and James M. Robins. *Causal Inference: What If*. Chapman and Hall/CRC, 2020.
  - [REF-MONTGOMERY-PECK-VINING-2021](../references.md#ref-montgomery-peck-vining-2021): Douglas C. Montgomery, Elizabeth A. Peck, and G. Geoffrey Vining. *Introduction to Linear Regression Analysis*. 6th edition. Wiley, 2021. ISBN 978-1-119-57872-7.
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-IV-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-iv-003--first-stage-f-statistic-of-the-excluded-instruments)

## `probstat.causal.omitted_variable_bias`

### EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-001 — Partial R-squared from a t statistic

`R^2_{Y~D|X} = t_D^2 / (t_D^2 + df)`, with `t_D` the classical t statistic
of `D` in the regression of `Y` on `(D, X)` and `df` its residual degrees of
freedom. Follows from `t_D^2 = df (RSS_X - RSS_{D,X}) / RSS_{D,X}`.

- Domain: least-squares regression with `df >= 1`.
- Meaning: share of the outcome variance not explained by `X` that `D`
  explains; also the strength with the treatment that a confounder
  explaining all residual outcome variance would need to remove the
  estimate.
- Code: `causal.partial_r2`, `causal.sensitivity_statistics`.
- Source: [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020]; re-derived and checked against the
  drop in residual sum of squares of fitted regressions.
  - [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020](../references.md#ref-cinelli-hazlett-2020): Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett. "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* 82(1), 39-67, 2020. Omitted-variable bias of a regression coefficient in terms of partial R-squared values, and the robustness value. DOI: [10.1111/rssb.12348](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12348).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-ovb-001--partial-r-squared-from-a-t-statistic)

### EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002 — Omitted-variable bias in partial R-squared form

For the long regression `Y = tau D + X'beta + gamma Z + e` and the short one
without `Z`, `tau_res - tau = gamma delta` with `delta` the coefficient of
`D` in the regression of `Z` on `(D, X)`, and

`|tau_res - tau| = se(tau_res) sqrt(df) sqrt(R^2_{Y~Z|D,X} R^2_{D~Z|X} /
(1 - R^2_{D~Z|X}))`,

with `se` and `df` from the short regression.

- Domain: `R^2_{D~Z|X} < 1`; classical (homoskedastic) standard error.
- Meaning: an exact sample identity, not a bound, when the two partial
  R-squared values are those of the omitted regressor; a bound on the bias
  when they are upper bounds on its strength. The sign of the bias is not
  determined by the partial R-squared values.
- Code: `causal.omitted_variable_bias`, `causal.adjust_for_confounder`.
- Source: [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020]; re-derived from the least-squares
  algebra and confirmed to `1e-12` by omitting a simulated confounder.
  - [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020](../references.md#ref-cinelli-hazlett-2020): Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett. "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* 82(1), 39-67, 2020. Omitted-variable bias of a regression coefficient in terms of partial R-squared values, and the robustness value. DOI: [10.1111/rssb.12348](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12348).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-ovb-002--omitted-variable-bias-in-partial-r-squared-form)

### EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-003 — Robustness value

`RV_q = (sqrt(f_q^4 + 4 f_q^2) - f_q^2) / 2`, `f_q = q |t_D| / sqrt(df)`:
the positive root of `R^2 + f_q^2 R - f_q^2 = 0`, i.e. of
`R / sqrt(1 - R) = f_q`.

- Domain: `q > 0`, `df >= 1`.
- Meaning: a confounder whose partial R-squared with both the treatment and
  the outcome equals `RV_q` changes the estimate by the fraction `q`
  (`q = 1`: to zero); confounders weaker than `RV_q` on both counts cannot.
- Code: `causal.robustness_value`, `causal.sensitivity_statistics`.
- Source: [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020]; follows from [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002] with
  equal strengths.
  - [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020](../references.md#ref-cinelli-hazlett-2020): Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett. "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* 82(1), 39-67, 2020. Omitted-variable bias of a regression coefficient in terms of partial R-squared values, and the robustness value. DOI: [10.1111/rssb.12348](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12348).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-ovb-003--robustness-value)

### EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-004 — Standard error and t statistic adjusted for a confounder

`se(tau) = se(tau_res) sqrt((1 - R^2_{Y~Z|D,X}) / (1 - R^2_{D~Z|X}))
sqrt(df / (df - 1))`, `tau = tau_res -+ |bias|` ([EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002]),
`t = tau / se(tau)` on `df - 1` degrees of freedom.

- Domain: `df >= 2`, `R^2_{D~Z|X} < 1`.
- Meaning: what the long regression would report; an exact sample identity
  for an actual omitted regressor.
- Code: `causal.adjust_for_confounder`.
- Source: [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020]; re-derived from
  `RSS_long = RSS_short (1 - R^2_{Y~Z|D,X})` and
  `SS(D | X, Z) = SS(D | X)(1 - R^2_{D~Z|X})`.
  - [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020](../references.md#ref-cinelli-hazlett-2020): Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett. "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* 82(1), 39-67, 2020. Omitted-variable bias of a regression coefficient in terms of partial R-squared values, and the robustness value. DOI: [10.1111/rssb.12348](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12348).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-004](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-ovb-004--standard-error-and-t-statistic-adjusted-for-a-confounder)

### EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-005 — Robustness value for statistical significance

With `f_q = q |t_D| / sqrt(df)` and `f* = t*_{alpha, df-1} / sqrt(df - 1)`
(`t*` the two-sided Student-t critical value):
`RV_{q,alpha} = 0` if `f_q <= f*`;
`RV_{q,alpha} =` [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-003] at `f_{q,alpha} = f_q - f*` if
`f* < f_q <= 1 / f*`;
`RV_{q,alpha} = (f_q^2 - f*^2) / (1 + f_q^2)` if `f_q > 1 / f*`.

- Domain: `df >= 2`, `0 < alpha < 1`.
- Meaning: the smallest `R` such that a confounder with
  `R^2_{Y~Z|D,X} <= R` and `R^2_{D~Z|X} <= R` can bring the adjusted t
  statistic for `H0: tau = (1 - q) tau_res` to the critical value. From
  [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002] and [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-004] the adjusted t over
  `sqrt(df - 1)` is `(f_q sqrt(1 - R2_D) - sqrt(R2_Y R2_D)) / sqrt(1 - R2_Y)`.
  It is minimized at equal strengths when `f_q <= 1 / f*`; otherwise at
  `R2_Y = R2_D / (f_q^2 (1 - R2_D)) < R2_D`, where it equals
  `sqrt(f_q^2 (1 - R2_D) - R2_D)`. The third branch is absent from the
  formula printed in the paper and present in the authors' `sensemakr`
  software (`robustness_value`); the equal-strength formula alone
  overstates the value there (0.983 instead of 0.964 at `t = 20`,
  `df = 5`).
- Code: `causal.robustness_value`, `causal.sensitivity_statistics`.
- Source: [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020]; derived here from
  [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-002] and [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-004] and checked by a grid search
  over both strengths.
  - [REF-CINELLI-HAZLETT-2020](../references.md#ref-cinelli-hazlett-2020): Carlos Cinelli and Chad Hazlett. "Making Sense of Sensitivity: Extending Omitted Variable Bias." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Statistical Methodology)* 82(1), 39-67, 2020. Omitted-variable bias of a regression coefficient in terms of partial R-squared values, and the robustness value. DOI: [10.1111/rssb.12348](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12348).
- Registry: [EQ-CAUSAL-OVB-005](../equation-registry.md#eq-causal-ovb-005--robustness-value-for-statistical-significance)

## `probstat.causal.sensitivity`

### EQ-CAUSAL-EVALUE-001 — E-value

For `RR >= 1`: `E = RR + sqrt(RR (RR - 1))`; for `RR < 1` apply to `1/RR`.
The interval E-value uses the limit closest to one and is `1` if the
interval contains one.

- Domain: `RR > 0` on the risk-ratio scale.
- Meaning: the minimum risk-ratio association an unmeasured confounder must
  have with both treatment and outcome to explain the estimate away.
- Code: `causal.e_value`.
- Source: [REF-VANDERWEELE-DING-2017].

`B = RR_EU RR_UD / (RR_EU + RR_UD - 1)`; `B(E, E) = RR` for the E-value of
[EQ-CAUSAL-EVALUE-001].

- Domain: `RR_EU >= 1`, `RR_UD >= 1`.
- Meaning: the largest factor by which a confounder of the given strengths
  can inflate a risk ratio.
- Code: `causal.confounding_bias_factor`.

## `probstat.causal.synthetic_control`

### EQ-CAUSAL-SC-001 — Synthetic control weights

`w* = argmin_w (x1 - X0 w)' V (x1 - X0 w)` subject to `w_j >= 0`,
`sum_j w_j = 1`, `V` diagonal and non-negative. With gradient
`g = -2 X0' V (x1 - X0 w)`, `w` is optimal iff `g_j = min_i g_i` whenever
`w_j > 0` (KKT), and `g'w - min_j g_j >= f(w) - f(w*)` for any feasible
`w`.

- Domain: `J >= 1` donors, `k >= 1` predictors; `V` user-supplied.
- Meaning: the convex combination of donors closest to the treated unit;
  unique fitted values, weights unique when `X0' V X0` is positive
  definite.
- Code: `causal.synthetic_control_weights`, `causal.synthetic_control`.

### EQ-CAUSAL-SC-002 — Euclidean projection onto the simplex
