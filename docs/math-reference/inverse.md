### EQ-KOH-MODEL-001 — Kennedy-O'Hagan observation model

`y_i = eta(c_i, theta) + delta(x_i) + eps_i`, `delta ~ GP(0, k_psi)`,
`eps_i ~ N(0, sigma_i^2)` independent of each other and of `delta`; `x_i` is
the subset of the conditions `c_i` the discrepancy depends on.

- Domain: one scalar observed quantity; simulator evaluated directly (no
  emulator); zero-mean discrepancy; no simulator scale factor `rho`.
- Meaning: the simulator at the best-fitting `theta` differs from reality by
  a smooth unknown function plus measurement error.
- Code: `inverse.DiscrepancyCalibration`,

### EQ-KOH-KERNEL-001 — Discrepancy covariance kernels

`k_psi(x, x') = sigma_delta^2 exp(-r^2/2)` (squared exponential) or
`sigma_delta^2 (1 + sqrt(5) r + 5 r^2/3) exp(-sqrt(5) r)` (Matern 5/2), with
`r^2 = sum_i ((x_i - x'_i)/ell_i)^2` and `psi = (sigma_delta, ell_1..ell_p)`.

- Domain: `sigma_delta >= 0`, `ell_i > 0`.
- Meaning: stationary prior on the discrepancy; `sigma_delta` sets its size,
  `ell_i` its smoothness scale along input `i`.
- Code: `inverse.discrepancy_kernel`,

### EQ-KOH-MARG-001 — Marginal likelihood with the discrepancy integrated out

`C_psi = K_psi + diag(sigma_i^2) = L L'`, `r = y - eta(C, theta)`,
`log p(y | theta, psi, sigma) = -(1/2)|L^-1 r|^2 - sum_i log L_ii
- (n/2) log 2pi`. Without an active discrepancy `C_psi` is diagonal and the
value is `sum_i -(1/2)(log 2pi + 2 log sigma_i + r_i^2/sigma_i^2)`.

- Domain: positive-definite `C_psi` (guaranteed in exact arithmetic when every
  `sigma_i > 0`); a failed factorization returns `-inf`. Cost `O(n^3)`.
- Meaning: `delta` is Gaussian and enters linearly, so it can be integrated
  out exactly; the remaining parameters are `(theta, psi, sigma)`.
- Code: `inverse.DiscrepancyCalibration.log_likelihood`,
  `inverse.DiscrepancyCalibration.marginal_covariance`.
- Source: [REF-KENNEDY-OHAGAN-2001] and

### EQ-KOH-PRED-001 — Bias-corrected and simulator-only prediction

Per draw `s`: `m_s = k*' C_psi^-1 r_s`, `v_s = k(x*, x*) - |L^-1 k*|^2`; the
bias-corrected process is `S^-1 sum_s N(eta(c*, theta_s) + m_s, v_s)` with
mean `mean_s(eta_s + m_s)` and variance `Var_s(eta_s + m_s) + mean_s(v_s)`;
the simulator alone is the sample `eta(c*, theta_s)`; a new observation adds
`sigma(c*)^2`. Interval limits invert the mixture CDF by bisection.

- Domain: positive-definite `C_psi` at every draw; marginal (pointwise)
  predictions only.
- Meaning: Gaussian conditioning of `delta(x*)` on the residuals, averaged
  over parameter uncertainty; the gap between the two predictions is the
  estimated discrepancy contribution.
- Code: `inverse.DiscrepancyCalibration.predict`,
  `inverse.DiscrepancyPrediction.interval`.

### EQ-CAL-LIK-001 — Gaussian and Student-t calibration likelihood

With total scale `s^2 = sigma_q^2 + sigma_delta_q^2` and residual `r`:
Gaussian `log p = -0.5 (log(2 pi s^2) + r^2/s^2)`; Student-t
`log p = log G((nu+1)/2) - log G(nu/2) - 0.5 log(nu pi s^2)
- (nu+1)/2 log(1 + r^2/(nu s^2))`; the log-likelihood is the sum over
observations.

- Domain: `sigma_q > 0`, `sigma_delta_q^2 >= 0`, `nu > 0`.
- Meaning: Gaussian noise or a heavy-tailed robust alternative; the
  discrepancy variance is a declared additive model-inadequacy term.

### EQ-CAL-STD-001 — Posterior in the prior's standard-normal space

For the Nataf map `u = T(theta)` of the prior,
`pi(T^-1(u)) |det dT^-1/du| = phi_d(u)`, so
`p(u, eta | y) proportional to L(T^-1(u), e^eta) phi_d(u)
prod_q p_sigma(e^eta_q) e^eta_q`.

- Domain: continuous prior marginals with a Gaussian copula; `u` in `R^d`.
- Meaning: sampling in `u` needs no explicit prior Jacobian; bounded or skewed
  priors become unbounded and roughly isotropic, which conditions the

### EQ-CAL-WLS-001 — Weighted calibration least squares

`theta_hat = argmin 0.5 sum_i (r_i(theta) / s_i)^2`, `s_i` the total noise

`s_q^2 = (RSS_q / n_q) n / (n - d)`.

- Domain: more observations than parameters when scales are estimated.
- Meaning: Gaussian maximum-likelihood estimate of `theta` (prior ignored)
  with Gauss-Newton covariance [EQ-OPT-LM-002] at unit weighted variance.

### EQ-CAL-FISHER-001 — Fisher-information identifiability spectrum

`F = J_w' J_w` with the Jacobian `J_w` of weighted predictions; analyse
`D F D = Q Lambda Q'` with `D = diag(scales)` (prior standard deviations by
default); `kappa = lambda_max / lambda_min`; eigenvectors with small
`lambda` are weakly identified parameter combinations.

- Domain: local linearization at a parameter point; Gaussian errors.
- Meaning: rank deficiency means non-identifiability; a large `kappa` means
  practical (weak) identifiability; prior-scaled eigenvalues compare data
  information with prior information.

### EQ-CAL-STD-002 — Fully standardized calibration coordinates

`v = (u, w)` with `theta = T^-1(u)` (Nataf map of the prior) and

- Meaning: the change of variables makes pCN and the SMC evidence

  are mapped back to `(u, log sigma)` and `(theta, sigma)`.

`y_i = f(x_i, theta) + delta_i + epsilon_i`, with
`Var(delta_i + epsilon_i) = sigma_delta^2 + sigma_epsilon^2` under independence.

## `probstat.inverse.regularization`

### EQ-INV-REG-001 — Tikhonov functional and its stacked least-squares form

`x_lambda = argmin_x ||A x - b||^2 + lambda^2 ||L (x - x_bar)||^2`, computed
as the least-squares solution of `[A; lambda L] x = [b; lambda L x_bar]`
(for the linear solver `x_bar = 0`) by Householder QR [EQ-LINALG-009]. For
`L = I` it equals the posterior mean of `x ~ N(0, (s^2 / lambda^2) I)` given
`b = A x + e`, `e ~ N(0, s^2 I)`. The nonlinear version minimizes
`||r(x)||^2 + lambda^2 ||L (x - x_bar)||^2` by Levenberg-Marquardt on the
stacked residual `[r(x); lambda L (x - x_bar)]` [EQ-OPT-LM-001].

- Domain: `lambda >= 0`; the null spaces of `A` and `L` intersect only in
  zero (otherwise the stacked matrix is rank deficient and the solve
  raises); `lambda = 0` needs `A` of full column rank.
- Meaning: the penalty selects, among the solutions that fit the data about
  equally well, one with small (semi)norm; `lambda` sets the trade-off. The
  normal equations `(A'A + lambda^2 L'L) x = A'b` are never formed, so the
  solve sees the condition number of the stacked matrix, not its square.
- Code: `inverse.tikhonov`, `inverse.nonlinear_tikhonov`,
  `inverse.regularization._stacked_qr`.
- Source: [REF-TIKHONOV-1963]; [REF-HANSEN-2008, Sections 2.2 and 2.7.1]
  (functional with `x_bar`, stacked form); [REF-TARANTOLA-2005, Chapter 3]
  (Gaussian interpretation).
  - [REF-TIKHONOV-1963](../references.md#ref-tikhonov-1963): Andrei N. Tikhonov. "On the Solution of Ill-Posed Problems and the Method of Regularization." *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR* 151(3), 501-504, 1963 (in Russian); English translation "Solution of Incorrectly Formulated Problems and the Regularization Method," *Soviet Mathematics Doklady* 4, 1035-1038, 1963. The penalized least-squares regularization named after him.
  - [REF-HANSEN-2008](../references.md#ref-hansen-2008): Per Christian Hansen. *Regularization Tools: A Matlab Package for Analysis and Solution of Discrete Ill-Posed Problems*, Version 4.1 for Matlab 7.3. ### EQ-INV-REG-002 — Tikhonov filter factors

For `L = I` and the SVD of `A`,
`x_lambda = sum_i f_i (u_i'b / sigma_i) v_i` with
`f_i = sigma_i^2 / (sigma_i^2 + lambda^2)` (`f_i = 0` when `sigma_i = 0`).

- Domain: `lambda >= 0`; the SVD of [EQ-LINALG-SVD-001].
- Meaning: components with `sigma_i >> lambda` pass almost unchanged
  (`f_i ~ 1`), those with `sigma_i << lambda` are damped (`f_i ~
  sigma_i^2 / lambda^2`); a discarded component still contributes about
  `sigma_i (u_i'b) / lambda^2`.

### EQ-INV-REG-003 — Generalized cross-validation function

`G(lambda) = ||A x_lambda - b||^2 / trace(I_m - A A^#)^2`, where
`x_lambda = A^# b`. For `L = I`, `trace(I_m - A A^#) = m - sum_i f_i`; for a
general `L`, with `[A; lambda L] = Q R` and `Q_1` the first `m` rows of `Q`,
`A A^# = Q_1 Q_1'`, so `trace(I_m - A A^#) = m - ||Q_1||_F^2`.

- Domain: `lambda > 0` on a grid (default geometric from
  `max(sigma_k, 16 eps sigma_1)` to `sigma_1`, divided by the extreme
  nonzero singular values of `L` for a general `L`); the grid minimum is
  refined by golden-section search in `log lambda`.
- Meaning: an estimate of the predictive risk that needs no noise level.
  For a square operator `G` tends to a finite limit as `lambda -> 0`, which
  can undercut the interior minimum and give a far too small `lambda`.
- Code: `inverse.generalized_cross_validation`, `inverse.regularization._gcv`,
  `inverse.regularization._Curve.point`.
- Source: [REF-CRAVEN-WAHBA-1979]; [REF-GOLUB-HEATH-WAHBA-1979];
  [REF-HANSEN-2008, Section 2.9, eqs. (2.62)-(2.63)]. The `Q_1` trace form
  follows from `A = Q_1 R` (derived for this library).

### EQ-INV-REG-004 — L-curve curvature

With `rho = ||A x_lambda - b||^2`, `eta = ||L x_lambda||^2`, and hats for
`log sqrt(.)`, the curvature of `(rho_hat, eta_hat)` is
`kappa = (rho_hat' eta_hat'' - rho_hat'' eta_hat') / ((rho_hat')^2 + (eta_hat')^2)^(3/2)`
(primes: derivatives in `lambda`). Using `rho' = -lambda^2 eta'` and
`eta' = (4 / lambda) (L x)'(L z)`, `z = (A'A + lambda^2 L'L)^-1 A'(A x - b)`
(for `L = I`: `eta' = -(4 / lambda) sum_i f_i^2 (1 - f_i) (u_i'b)^2 / sigma_i^2`),
`kappa = 2 rho eta (lambda^2 eta' rho + 2 lambda rho eta + lambda^4 eta eta')
/ (|eta'| (lambda^4 eta^2 + rho^2)^(3/2))`.

- Domain: `lambda > 0` with `rho, eta, eta' != 0` (otherwise `nan`); same
  grid and refinement as [EQ-INV-REG-003], maximizing `kappa`.
- Meaning: the corner where the curve turns from the steep branch (noise
  dominated) to the flat branch (regularization dominated). The closed form
  is checked against finite differences of the log norms in the tests.
- Code: `inverse.l_curve`, `inverse.regularization._curvature`,
  `inverse.regularization._Curve.point`.
- Source: [REF-HANSEN-OLEARY-1993, Section 5.1, eq. (8)] (curvature in log
  norms, corner at maximum curvature); [REF-HANSEN-2008, Sections 2.5 and
  2.9]. The reduction to `rho`, `eta`, `eta'` is derived for this library
  from `x' = (2 / lambda) z`.

"The Use of the L-Curve in the Regularization of Discrete Ill-Posed Problems." *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* 14(6), 1487-1503, 1993. ### EQ-INV-REG-005 — Discrepancy principle

Choose `lambda` with `||A x_lambda - b|| = tau delta`, `delta ~ ||e||`,
`tau >= 1`; the root is found by Brent's method [EQ-OPT-BRENT-001] in
`log lambda` after bracketing on the grid.

- Domain: `||A x_lambda - b||` increases from the least-squares residual
  (`lambda -> 0`) to `||b||` (`lambda -> inf`, for `L = I`); a target
  outside that range raises `ValueError`.
- Meaning: fit the data no better than the noise allows. An underestimated
  `delta` gives an under-regularized solution with a large norm, an
  overestimated one an over-smoothed solution.
- Code: `inverse.discrepancy_principle`.
- Source: [REF-MOROZOV-1966]; [REF-HANSEN-2008, Section 2.9, eq. (2.61)].

### EQ-INV-REG-PROB-001 — Discrete backward heat operator
