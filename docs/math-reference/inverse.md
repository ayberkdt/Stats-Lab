### EQ-INV-CBS-002 — Consensus-based sampling update

`u_j <- m_b + alpha (u_j - m_b) + sqrt((1 - alpha^2) / lambda) C_b^1/2 xi_j`,

- Domain: `0 <= alpha < 1`; `C_b` positive definite.

### EQ-INV-CBS-003 — Gaussian fixed point of consensus-based sampling

If the ensemble law is `N(m, C)` and
`Phi(u) = (u - mu)' Sigma^-1 (u - mu) / 2 + const`, then
`C_b^-1 = C^-1 + beta Sigma^-1`, `m_b = C_b (C^-1 m + beta Sigma^-1 mu)`, and
the update of [EQ-INV-CBS-002] gives the law
`N(m_b + alpha (m - m_b), alpha^2 C + (1 - alpha^2) C_b / lambda)`.

- Domain: mean-field (infinite-ensemble) iteration; quadratic `Phi`.
- Meaning: a fixed point needs `m = mu` and
  `(1 - lambda) C^-1 = lambda beta Sigma^-1`. With `lambda = 1 / (1 + beta)`
  this is `C = Sigma` for every `beta`: the posterior `N(mu, Sigma)` is
  stationary (the reweighted covariance of the target is
  `Sigma / (1 + beta)`). With `lambda = 1` only `C = 0` is fixed and the
  mean tends to the minimizer `mu`. For `J` members `C_b` is low by about
  `1 / J_eff`, which shifts the fixed point to about
  `(1 - (1 + beta) / (beta J_eff)) Sigma`. For a non-Gaussian posterior the
  method is an approximation.
- Code: `inverse.consensus_based_sampling`.
- Source: derived in ADR-0130; Gaussian steady states are the subject of
  [REF-CARRILLO-ETAL-2022].
  - [REF-CARRILLO-ETAL-2022](../references.md#ref-carrillo-etal-2022): José A. Carrillo, Franca Hoffmann, Andrew M. Stuart, and Urbain Vaes. "Consensus-Based Sampling." *Studies in Applied Mathematics* 148(3), 1069-1140, 2022. Weighted-ensemble dynamics with a sampling and an optimization mode and Gaussian steady states. DOI: [10.1111/sapm.12470](https://doi.org/10.1111/sapm.12470).
- Registry: [EQ-INV-CBS-003](../equation-registry.md#eq-inv-cbs-003--gaussian-fixed-point-of-consensus-based-sampling)

## `probstat.inverse.ensemble_inversion`

### EQ-INV-EKI-001 — Ensemble Kalman inversion update

For members `u_j`, `j = 1..N`, in the prior's standard-normal coordinates
with predictions `g_j = G(T^-1(u_j))`, ensemble means `u_bar`, `g_bar` and
`C^up = sum_j (u_j - u_bar)(g_j - g_bar)' / (N - 1)`,
`C^pp = sum_j (g_j - g_bar)(g_j - g_bar)' / (N - 1)`:
`u_j <- u_j + C^up (C^pp + Gamma)^-1 (y + eta_j - g_j)` with
`eta_j ~ N(0, Gamma)` drawn afresh at every iteration (or `eta_j = 0`
without perturbation). In whitened form, with `A` and `B` the deviations of
`u` and of `Gamma^-1/2 g` from their means,
`C^up (C^pp + alpha Gamma)^-1 = A'B (B'B + alpha (N - 1) I)^-1 Gamma^-1/2
= A' (B B' + alpha (N - 1) I)^-1 B Gamma^-1/2`.

- Domain: `N >= 2`; `Gamma` symmetric positive definite (here diagonal with
  entries `sigma_q^2 + sigma_delta_q^2` of known Gaussian noise); `alpha > 0`.
- Meaning: a derivative-free iteration that decreases the data misfit; each
  member stays in the affine span of the initial ensemble and the ensemble
  covariance collapses as the iterations proceed, so the method is an
  optimizer and iterative regularizer, not a posterior sampler. Iglesias,
  Law and Stuart normalize the covariances by `N`; this library uses
  `N - 1` in both, as in the ensemble Kalman filter.
- Code: `inverse.ensemble_kalman_inversion`, `inverse.ensemble_smoother_mda`.
- Source: [REF-IGLESIAS-LAW-STUART-2013, Algorithm 1];
  [REF-SCHILLINGS-STUART-2017] (gradient-flow limit and collapse);
  [REF-EVENSEN-2003] (ensemble gain).
  - [REF-IGLESIAS-LAW-STUART-2013](../references.md#ref-iglesias-law-stuart-2013): Marco A. Iglesias, Kody J. H. Law, and Andrew M. Stuart. "Ensemble Kalman Methods for Inverse Problems." *Inverse Problems* 29(4), 045001, 2013. The iterative ensemble Kalman method with perturbed data `y + eta_j`, `eta_j ~ N(0, Gamma)`, the invariant-subspace property of the ensemble, and its use as a derivative-free optimizer (Algorithm 1, Section 2). DOI: [10.1088/0266-5611/29/4/045001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/29/4/045001).
  - [REF-SCHILLINGS-STUART-2017](../references.md#ref-schillings-stuart-2017): Claudia Schillings and Andrew M. Stuart. ### EQ-INV-EKI-002 — Discrepancy-principle stopping of ensemble Kalman inversion

`|Gamma^-1/2 (y - g_bar_n)| <= tau eta`, where `g_bar_n` is the ensemble
mean of the predictions at iteration `n`, `eta = |Gamma^-1/2 (y - G(u_true))|`
is the noise level, and `tau > 1`.

- Domain: `tau >= 1` (enforced); `eta > 0`. When the noise realization is
  unknown the library uses `eta = sqrt(M)` for `M` observations, the root of

  a library choice.
- Meaning: iterating past the noise level fits the noise of an ill-posed
  problem; stopping there makes the iteration a regularization method. The
  criterion tests the mean of the predictions, not the prediction at the
  mean, so it needs no extra forward run.

### EQ-INV-ALDI-001 — Affine-invariant interacting Langevin dynamics

For members `u_j`, `j = 1..J`, in `R^d` with mean `m`, covariance
`C = sum_k (u_k - m)(u_k - m)' / J` and potential
`Phi(u) = |Gamma^-1/2 (y - G(u))|^2 / 2 + |u|^2 / 2`:
`du_j = -C grad Phi(u_j) dt + ((d + 1)/J)(u_j - m) dt + sqrt(2) C^1/2 dW_j`,
with `C^1/2 = (u_1 - m, ..., u_J - m) / sqrt(J)` a `d x J` matrix and `W_j`
independent `J`-dimensional Brownian motions. The correction is the
divergence `div_{u_j} C = ((d + 1)/J)(u_j - m)`.

- Domain: `J >= d + 2` (non-singular `C`); prior `N(0, I)` in `u`; `Gamma`
  symmetric positive definite (here diagonal, known Gaussian noise).
- Meaning: the noise of member `j` has covariance `2 C dt` and is
  independent across members, so the probability flux of member `j` in the
  Fokker-Planck equation of the ensemble is
  `rho (-C grad_j Phi + div_j C) - div_j (C rho) = -rho C grad_j Phi - C grad_j rho`,
  which vanishes at `rho = prod_j exp(-Phi(u_j))`: the product of posteriors
  is invariant at every finite ensemble size. The dynamics is invariant
  under affine changes of `u`, so its relaxation rate does not depend on the
  conditioning of the posterior.
- Code: `inverse.interacting_langevin_sampler`.
- Source: [REF-GARBUNO-INIGO-NUSKEN-REICH-2020] (corrected dynamics);
  [REF-GARBUNO-INIGO-ETAL-2020] (dynamics without the correction). The flux
  argument is rederived in ADR-0130.

### EQ-INV-ALDI-003 — Euler-Maruyama step, step rule and time averages

`u_j <- u_j - h_n [C^ug Gamma^-1 (g_j - y) + C u_j - kappa (u_j - m)]

`h_n = eta / (tr C + tr C^gg)` with
`C^gg = sum_k Gamma^-1/2 (g_k - g_bar)(g_k - g_bar)' Gamma^-1/2 / J`.

- Domain: `0 < eta < 2`, or a fixed `h > 0`.
- Meaning: for an affine map the drift matrix is `C (I + A' Gamma^-1 A)`,

  `h_n lambda_max <= eta` and the deterministic part of the step is stable;
  in stationarity `h_n ~ eta / d`. A heuristic, not an error-controlled
  integrator: the discrete chain has an `O(h)` bias, and because `h_n`
  depends on the state the averages are weighted by `h_n`.

### EQ-INV-ALDI-004 — Covariance deficit without the finite-ensemble correction

Without the term `((d + 1)/J)(u_j - m)` and for a Gaussian posterior
`N(mu, Sigma)` the invariant density of the ensemble is
`prod_j pi(u_j) det(C)^(-(d + 1)/2)`, so `J C ~ Wishart(Sigma, J - d - 2)`,
`m ~ N(mu, Sigma / J)` independently, `E[C] = ((J - d - 2)/J) Sigma`, and the
pooled covariance of the members is `((J - d - 1)/J) Sigma`.

- Domain: affine forward map; `J > 2 d + 1` for a normalizable density.
- Meaning: with `rho = prod_j pi(u_j) phi(U)` the flux of

  posterior covariance, by half for `J = 6`, `d = 2`; the deficit vanishes
  as `J -> infinity`.

### EQ-INV-REG-001 — Tikhonov functional and its stacked least-squares form

`x_lambda = argmin_x ||A x - b||^2 + lambda^2 ||L (x - x_bar)||^2`, computed
as the least-squares solution of `[A; lambda L] x = [b; lambda L x_bar]`
(for the linear solver `x_bar = 0`) by Householder QR [EQ-LINALG-009]. For
`L = I` it equals the posterior mean of `x ~ N(0, (s^2 / lambda^2) I)` given
`b = A x + e`, `e ~ N(0, s^2 I)`. The nonlinear version minimizes
`||r(x)||^2 + lambda^2 ||L (x - x_bar)||^2` by Levenberg-Marquardt on the
stacked residual `[r(x); lambda L (x - x_bar)]` [EQ-OPT-LM-001].

- Domain: `lambda >= 0`; the null spaces of `A` and `L` intersect only in
  zero (otherwise the stacked matrix is rank deficient and the solve
  raises); `lambda = 0` needs `A` of full column rank.
- Meaning: the penalty selects, among the solutions that fit the data about
  equally well, one with small (semi)norm; `lambda` sets the trade-off. The
  normal equations `(A'A + lambda^2 L'L) x = A'b` are never formed, so the
  solve sees the condition number of the stacked matrix, not its square.
- Code: `inverse.tikhonov`, `inverse.nonlinear_tikhonov`,
  `inverse.regularization._stacked_qr`.
- Source: [REF-TIKHONOV-1963]; [REF-HANSEN-2008, Sections 2.2 and 2.7.1]
  (functional with `x_bar`, stacked form); [REF-TARANTOLA-2005, Chapter 3]
  (Gaussian interpretation).

### EQ-INV-REG-002 — Tikhonov filter factors

For `L = I` and the SVD of `A`,
`x_lambda = sum_i f_i (u_i'b / sigma_i) v_i` with
`f_i = sigma_i^2 / (sigma_i^2 + lambda^2)` (`f_i = 0` when `sigma_i = 0`).

### EQ-INV-REG-003 — Generalized cross-validation function

- Meaning: an estimate of the predictive risk that needs no noise level.

### EQ-INV-REG-004 — L-curve curvature

`log sqrt(.)`, the curvature of `(rho_hat, eta_hat)` is
`kappa = (rho_hat' eta_hat'' - rho_hat'' eta_hat') / ((rho_hat')^2 + (eta_hat')^2)^(3/2)`
(primes: derivatives in `lambda`). Using `rho' = -lambda^2 eta'` and
`eta' = (4 / lambda) (L x)'(L z)`, `z = (A'A + lambda^2 L'L)^-1 A'(A x - b)`

`kappa = 2 rho eta (lambda^2 eta' rho + 2 lambda rho eta + lambda^4 eta eta')
/ (|eta'| (lambda^4 eta^2 + rho^2)^(3/2))`.

- Domain: `lambda > 0` with `rho, eta, eta' != 0` (otherwise `nan`); same
  grid and refinement as [EQ-INV-REG-003], maximizing `kappa`.
- Meaning: the corner where the curve turns from the steep branch (noise
  dominated) to the flat branch (regularization dominated). The closed form

### EQ-INV-REG-005 — Discrepancy principle

### EQ-INV-SPEC-001 — Whitened singular spectrum, nullspace and Picard coefficients

`W A = U diag(sigma) V'`; rank `r = #{sigma_i > max(atol, rtol sigma_1)}`;
`P_null = I - V_r V_r'` with a completed orthonormal basis of the full
nullspace; Picard coefficients `|u_i'W y|` and `|u_i'W y| / sigma_i`;
out-of-range data `||(I - U U')W y||`; noise amplification `1/sigma_i`.

- Domain: dense operator (oracle scale); `sigma_i = 0` handled without
  division.
- Meaning: which parameter combinations the data can resolve. The numerical
  cutoff is not a detection threshold: a coefficient `c` along `v_i` changes
  the whitened data by `c sigma_i`, detectable against unit noise only when
  `|c| sigma_i >~ 1` (heuristic). Repeated singular values define subspaces,
  not unique vectors.

## `probstat.inverse.unscented_inversion`

### EQ-INV-UKI-001 — Unscented Kalman inversion iteration

Prediction `m^ = m_n`, `C^ = 2 C_n`. Analysis with the augmented map
`G_a(u) = (G(u), u)`, data `y_a = (y, 0)` and `Gamma_a = diag(Gamma, I)`:
`m_{n+1} = m^ + C^uy (C^yy + 2 Gamma_a)^-1 (y_a - y^)`,
`C_{n+1} = C^ - C^uy (C^yy + 2 Gamma_a)^-1 C^uy'`, where `y^`, `C^uy`, `C^yy`
are the sigma-point moments of `G_a` under `N(m^, C^)`
([EQ-INV-UKI-002]). Start: `m_0 = 0`, `C_0 = I` (the prior).

- Domain: prior `N(0, I)` in `u`; `Gamma` positive definite.
- Meaning: the general scheme `m^ = alpha m + (1 - alpha) r_0`,
  `C^ = alpha^2 C + Sigma_w`, analysis with `Sigma_nu`, specialized to
  `alpha = 1`, `Sigma_w = C_n`, `Sigma_nu = 2 Gamma_a`, the choice for which
  the fixed point is the posterior in the affine case
  ([EQ-INV-UKI-003]). Each iteration costs `2 d + 1` forward evaluations.
- Code: `inverse.unscented_kalman_inversion`.
- Source: [REF-HUANG-SCHNEIDER-STUART-2022]; the hyperparameters are
  rederived in ADR-0130.
  - [REF-HUANG-SCHNEIDER-STUART-2022](../references.md#ref-huang-schneider-stuart-2022): Daniel Zhengyu Huang, Tapio Schneider, and Andrew M. Stuart. "Iterated Kalman Methodology for Inverse Problems." *Journal of Computational Physics* 463, 111262, 2022. ### EQ-INV-UKI-002 — Sigma points and weights of the unscented transform

For `N(m, C)` with `C = L L'`: points `m` and `m +/- sqrt(d + kappa) L e_i`,
`i = 1..d`; weights `kappa / (d + kappa)` for the centre and
`1 / (2 (d + kappa))` for the others, used for means and covariances alike.

- Domain: `kappa >= 0` (non-negative weights); `C` positive definite.
- Meaning: the weighted mean and covariance of the points are `m` and `C`
  exactly, so the moments of an affine map are exact; for a nonlinear map
  the transform matches the Taylor expansion of the mean to second order.
- Code: `inverse.unscented_kalman_inversion`.

### EQ-INV-UKI-003 — Linear fixed point of unscented Kalman inversion

For `G(u) = A u + b`, `P = A' Gamma^-1 A + I`:
`C_{n+1}^-1 = (C_n^-1 + P) / 2` and
`C_{n+1}^-1 m_{n+1} = (C_n^-1 m_n + A' Gamma^-1 (y - b)) / 2`.

- Domain: affine forward map.
- Meaning: the Kalman analysis in information form is
  `C_{n+1}^-1 = (2 C_n)^-1 + A_a' (2 Gamma_a)^-1 A_a` with
  `A_a' Gamma_a^-1 A_a = P`. The unique fixed point is `C = P^-1`,
  `m = P^-1 A' Gamma^-1 (y - b)`, the posterior covariance and mean, and the
  errors of the precision and of the precision-weighted mean halve at every
  iteration. With `alpha < 1` and `r_0 = 0` the same computation gives the
  fixed-point mean `P^-1 A' Gamma^-1 (y - b) / (2 - alpha)`, which is why
  `alpha = 1` is fixed.
- Code: `inverse.unscented_kalman_inversion`.
- Source: derived in ADR-0130 for the scheme of
  [REF-HUANG-SCHNEIDER-STUART-2022].
  - [REF-HUANG-SCHNEIDER-STUART-2022](../references.md#ref-huang-schneider-stuart-2022): Daniel Zhengyu Huang, Tapio Schneider, and Andrew M. Stuart. "Iterated Kalman Methodology for Inverse Problems." *Journal of Computational Physics* 463, 111262, 2022. Unscented Kalman inversion: a Gaussian approximation iterated by a prediction step and a sigma-point Kalman analysis step. DOI: [10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111262](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111262).
