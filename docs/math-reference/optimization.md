### EQ-OPT-FD-001 — Central difference derivative

`df/dx_i ~= [f(x + h e_i) - f(x - h e_i)] / (2h)`, `h = eps_rel max(1,|x_i|)`.

- Domain: `f` continuously differentiable near `x`; truncation error
  `O(h^2)` plus rounding error `O(eps/h)`.
- Meaning: gradient and Jacobian approximations for optimization, FORM, and
  Laplace approximations; each costs `2n` evaluations.
- Code: `optimization.central_gradient`, `optimization.central_jacobian`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 8.1].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-FD-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-fd-001--central-difference-derivative)

### EQ-OPT-FD-002 — Central difference Hessian

`H_ii ~= [f(x+h_i e_i) - 2f(x) + f(x-h_i e_i)]/h_i^2` and
`H_ij ~= [f(++) - f(+-) - f(-+) + f(--)]/(4 h_i h_j)`.

- Domain: `f` twice continuously differentiable near `x`.
- Meaning: curvature for SORM, Laplace posterior covariances, and
  diagnostics; costs `1 + 2n + 4n(n-1)/2` evaluations.
- Code: `optimization.central_hessian`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 8.1].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-FD-002](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-fd-002--central-difference-hessian)

### EQ-OPT-ARMIJO-001 — Armijo sufficient decrease

Accept step `alpha` when `f(x + alpha p) <= f(x) + c alpha grad f(x)' p` with
`c = 1e-4`, halving `alpha` otherwise.

- Domain: descent direction `grad f(x)' p < 0`.
- Meaning: guarantees monotone objective decrease in line-search methods.
- Code: `optimization.minimize_bfgs`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 3.1].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-ARMIJO-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-armijo-001--armijo-sufficient-decrease)

### EQ-OPT-BFGS-001 — BFGS inverse-Hessian update

`H_(k+1) = (I - rho s y') H_k (I - rho y s') + rho s s'`, `rho = 1/(y's)`,
`s = x_(k+1) - x_k`, `y = grad_(k+1) - grad_k`.

- Domain: curvature condition `y's > 0`; otherwise the update is skipped.
- Meaning: superlinearly convergent quasi-Newton minimization of smooth
  objectives.
- Code: `optimization.minimize_bfgs`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 6.1].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-BFGS-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-bfgs-001--bfgs-inverse-hessian-update)

### EQ-OPT-NM-001 — Nelder-Mead simplex moves

With centroid `c` of the best `n` vertices and worst vertex `w`, trial points
are `c + t(c - w)` for reflection `t=1`, expansion `t=2`, outside/inside
contraction `t=+-1/2`, followed by shrinkage toward the best vertex.

- Domain: any objective; no convergence guarantee in general.
- Meaning: derivative-free minimization for noisy or non-smooth objectives.
- Code: `optimization.minimize_nelder_mead`.
- Source: [REF-NELDER-MEAD-1965].
  - [REF-NELDER-MEAD-1965](../references.md#ref-nelder-mead-1965): J. A. Nelder and R. Mead. "A Simplex Method for Function Minimization." *The Computer Journal* 7(4), 308-313, 1965.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-NM-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-nm-001--nelder-mead-simplex-moves)

### EQ-OPT-LM-001 — Levenberg-Marquardt step

`(J'J + lambda diag(J'J)) delta = -J'r`; accept when the cost
`0.5||r||^2` decreases, then shrink `lambda`, otherwise enlarge it.

- Domain: residual vector with at least as many components as parameters.
- Meaning: interpolates between Gauss-Newton and scaled gradient descent for
  nonlinear least squares and calibration.
- Code: `optimization.least_squares`.
- Source: [REF-MORE-1978] and [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 10.3].
  - [REF-MORE-1978](../references.md#ref-more-1978): Jorge J. Moré. "The Levenberg-Marquardt Algorithm: Implementation and Theory." In *Numerical Analysis*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 630, Springer, 105-116, 1978.

### EQ-OPT-LM-002 — Gauss-Newton asymptotic covariance

### EQ-OPT-BRENT-001 — Brent root finding

Combine inverse quadratic interpolation, secant steps, and bisection while

### EQ-OPT-FD-003 — Relative finite-difference steps

`h_i = c max(|x_i|, typ_i)` with `c = sqrt(eps) ~ 1.5e-8` (forward) or
`c = eps^(1/3) ~ 6.1e-6` (central), rounded so that `x_i + h_i` is exactly
representable; `typ_i` is a typical magnitude (default 1, or `|x0_i|` inside
BFGS). The rounding error of a central quotient is about `eps |f| / h_i`.

- Domain: `f` smooth near `x`; supersedes the step quoted in
  [EQ-OPT-FD-001] for the default; an explicit `relative_step` keeps
  `h = c max(1, |x_i|)` and an explicit `step` is used as given.
- Meaning: steps relative to the variable, balancing truncation
  (`O(h)` forward, `O(h^2)` central) against rounding (`O(eps/h)`).
- Code: `optimization.central_gradient`, `optimization.forward_gradient`,
  `optimization.central_jacobian`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 8.1], [REF-DENNIS-SCHNABEL-1996,
  Section 5.4].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.

Classics in Applied Mathematics 16, SIAM, Philadelphia, 1996 (unabridged republication of the 1983 Prentice-Hall edition).

### EQ-OPT-FD-004 — One-sided second-order difference

`f'(x) ~= s (-3 f(x) + 4 f(x + s h) - f(x + 2 s h)) / (2 h)`, `s = +-1`;
first order `s (f(x + s h) - f(x)) / h` when `x + 2 s h` also fails.

- Domain: `f` defined on the chosen side; used when `f(x -+ h)` raises or is
  non-finite (domain boundaries); error `O(h^2)`.
- Meaning: gradients next to a domain boundary without leaving the domain.
- Code: `optimization.central_gradient`, `optimization.forward_gradient`.

### EQ-OPT-WOLFE-001 — Strong Wolfe conditions

Accept `alpha` when `f(x + alpha p) <= f(x) + c1 alpha g'p` and
`|grad f(x + alpha p)' p| <= c2 |g'p|`, `c1 = 1e-4`, `c2 = 0.9`; found by
bracketing (doubling) and zoom with safeguarded cubic or quadratic
interpolation.

- Domain: descent direction `g'p < 0`, `f` bounded below along `p`.
- Meaning: guarantees `y's > 0`, so the BFGS update stays positive definite.
- Code: `optimization.minimize_bfgs`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Section 3.5, Algorithms 3.5-3.6].
  - [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006](../references.md#ref-nocedal-wright-2006): Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright. *Numerical Optimization*. 2nd edition. Springer, 2006.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-WOLFE-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-wolfe-001--strong-wolfe-conditions)

### EQ-OPT-BFGS-002 — Initial inverse-Hessian scaling and stopping rules

Before the first update `H_0 = (s'y / y'y) I`. Stop when `||g||_2 <= tol`
(absolute), or when `max_i |s_i| / max(|x_i|, typ_i) <= eps^(2/3)`, or when a
line search fails with predicted decrease `-g'p/2 <= 16 eps |f|`; a
finite-difference gradient also stops at `||g|| <= ||(eps |f| / h_i)_i||`
when that resolution exceeds `tol`. Any other line-search failure, and the
iteration limit, are reported as not converged.

- Domain: smooth objectives; the tolerances do not depend on an additive
  constant in `f`; only the two floating-point resolution limits involve
  `eps |f|`.
- Meaning: scale-aware first step and honest convergence reporting.
- Code: `optimization.minimize_bfgs`.
- Source: [REF-NOCEDAL-WRIGHT-2006, Equation 6.20], [REF-DENNIS-SCHNABEL-1996,
  Section 7.2].

Classics in Applied Mathematics 16, SIAM, Philadelphia, 1996 (unabridged republication of the 1983 Prentice-Hall edition).

### EQ-OPT-BOX-001 — Box-constraint transformations

`x = l + (u - l)(1 + sin z)/2` (two bounds), `x = l - 1 + sqrt(z^2 + 1)`
(lower only), `x = u + 1 - sqrt(z^2 + 1)` (upper only); the optimizer works
in `z` with gradient `(dF/dx)(dx/dz)`.

- Domain: `l < u`; start points on a bound are moved inward by `1e-3` of the
  width (or of `max(1, |bound|)`) because `dx/dz = 0` there.
- Meaning: unconstrained minimization whose every evaluation lies in the box;
  a minimum on a bound is a stationary point in `z`.
- Code: `optimization.BoxTransform`, `optimization.minimize_bfgs`,
  `optimization.minimize_nelder_mead`, `optimization.multi_start`.
- Source: [REF-JAMES-ROOS-1975].
  - [REF-JAMES-ROOS-1975](../references.md#ref-james-roos-1975): F. James and M. Roos. "Minuit - A System for Function Minimization and Analysis of the Parameter Errors and Correlations." *Computer Physics Communications* 10(6), 343-367, 1975.
- Registry: [EQ-OPT-BOX-001](../equation-registry.md#eq-opt-box-001--box-constraint-transformations)

### EQ-OPT-ADAM-002 — Adam descent step

`m_t = b1 m_{t-1} + (1 - b1) g_t`, `v_t = b2 v_{t-1} + (1 - b2) g_t^2`,
`theta_t = theta_{t-1} - alpha (m_t / (1 - b1^t)) / (sqrt(v_t / (1 - b2^t)) + eps)`;
optionally the reported parameters average the iterates of the final
fraction of the iterations.

- Domain: stochastic gradients `g_t` of the objective to minimize;
  `0 <= b1, b2 < 1`, `alpha, eps > 0`.
- Meaning: the minimization form of [EQ-OPT-ADAM-001]; tail averaging
  reduces the noise a constant step leaves around the optimum.
- Code: `optimization.adam_minimize`.
- Source: [REF-KINGMA-BA-2015, Algorithm 1] and [REF-POLYAK-JUDITSKY-1992].
  - [REF-KINGMA-BA-2015](../references.md#ref-kingma-ba-2015): Diederik P. Kingma and Jimmy Ba. "Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization." In *Proceedings of the 3rd International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR)*, 2015. [arXiv:1412.6980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6980).
  - [REF-POLYAK-JUDITSKY-1992](../references.md#ref-polyak-juditsky-1992): B. T. Polyak and A. B. Juditsky. "Acceleration of Stochastic Approximation by Averaging." *SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization* 30(4), 838-855, 1992. DOI: [10.1137/0330046](https://doi.org/10.1137/0330046).
